"Time travel, I maintain, is possible."

An issue with how a supposed time traveler can travel for, lets say, 1 hour, and appear months or years in the past. It appears as though potentially two different dimensions of time are present, one for the regular human and one for the time traveler - as such, ==travel duration== is an important consideration, whether it takes a given interval or is instantaneous is up in the air.

It is easier to argue that time is one dimensional, one of the 4 dimensions in which we occupy (the other three being the spatial dimensions)
*Change* is qualitative difference between temporal stages
	Thus, a number (the form of a number) doesn't truly change over time
	However, a person does (its structure, memory, etc.)

*External time* - that which occupies a certain role in the pattern of events that comprise the course of the universe

*Personal time* - that which occupies a certain role in the pattern of events that comprise the time travelers life
	Properties continue to change consistently
Time travelers travel across the line of time in a ne dimensional zig-zag, overlapping themselves over and over

If there is a disagreement between personal and external time then time travel is occurring (so simply existing doing nothing is not considered time travel)

*Extended personal time* is the summation of the two, equating all of the time that has passed total

An event in external time can be experienced across an individual's personal time more than once

Time travel appears as a zig-zagging distance across a single line of time, stretching or shrinking personal time within the context of external time. Note these are not two independent dimensions (just as two objects changing position across the x axis aren't orthogonal to one another, yet merely occupying and interacting with the dimension on different levels)

Shouldn't we treat a time traveler talking to themself over the phone as two different people talking to one another? Lewis argues no, as there exists a sort of causal continuity between the two (that can be seen through memory) that does not necessarily exist with two separate entities.

*Tim* and **Tom**:
Lewis asks you to imagine a kid, a very spiteful kid, who really hates his grandfather. He hates his grandfather so much, even after his grandfather's death, that he uses his grandfather's fortune from being a munitions plant owner to build a time machine and try to kill him before he had died.
All of the pieces are put into play: is unwavering determination, his training and prepwork, the positioning of the target, etc. The question remains: can Tim kill his grandfather.
Lewis says he both can - as he is the most capable as anyone could ever be to kill him - and also can't - as his presence in the past is causally dependent on his grandfather being alive. Lewis says this also isn't a problem, as people say they can and can't all the time. E.g. I can speak Finnish, while an ape can't (due to our different laryngeal anatomy), but I can't speak Finnish.
	The issue is whether can coincides with the relevant set of facts (physical facts, potential facts)
Lewis also proposes another individual, **Tom**, want's to kill the grandfather's partner. His motivations, planning, and even his convictions of himself as a time traveler are all the same. He just isn't a time traveler. Can **Tom** kill his partner? The answer seems to be a quite clear yes. But now there is another issue that arises (can you see it?) 
What is the difference between *Tim* and **Tom**? It is mostly the causal relationship and logical necessity of the grandfathers life. Now, there could be a branching timeline from this point, though I find that to be quite lame.
