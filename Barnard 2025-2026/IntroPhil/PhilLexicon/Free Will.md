## Thought Cabinet
An argument against the incompatibility of free will and determinism: 
	When we think of determinism, of determining the next state of the world based off the current state of the world and the laws of nature, we think that this is near indisputable proof that we have no control. If it is the laws that are controlling us, and if everything is causally linked, then where could our free will possibly be?
	But, there is an error in this sort of thinking, and that is because the laws of nature that we describe aren't laws of governance, like we tend to think of laws to be (where if you attempt break them, there appears to be some sort of punishment, or even some sort of impasse). The electrons aren't going to send you to jail for any reason or another. The laws, then, are not rules of universal governance, but merely descriptions of the world around us. The particles do not follow the rules, they can merely be represented using maths. This leaves room for us humans to be movers of a sort, not some external rules that exist outside the universe (somehow not causally linked themselves). In a way, this makes more sense. Perhaps we have just deluded ourselves into thinking otherwise, a victim narrative of sorts - a slave morality - that rewards the inability to change where you are fated to be.
