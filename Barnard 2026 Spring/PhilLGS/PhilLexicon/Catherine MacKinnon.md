Discusses the topics of [[Rape]], [[Pornography]], objectification, Marxism, sexualization and the institution of sexuality (a. la. [[Michel Foucault]]) 

In "==Feminism, Marxism, Method, and the State: An Agenda for Theory==," identifies that the root of struggle in feminist theory at the time is centered around sexuality. It is to feminism what work is to Marxism. Sexuality is exploited, taken from the woman by the man in order to maintain centers of power. Sexuality converges at issues of rape, pornography, prostitution, marriage, reproduction, sexual health, labour, and so on. It is so pertinent that women are subjects to the institution of sexuality that lesbians are often considered un-woman. It seems like (one's relation to) sexuality determines gender, not the other way around. 
Rather than violence, economics, or authority, it is sexuality (a system of power) that enforces and constructs the female subject. Eroticism is coercion. Sexuality is a necessary constituent of gender, for gender is determined by one's relationship to sexuality. How does one sell their sexuality? How does one react to their sexuality being taken? Does their position in society allow for their sexuality to be traded, sold, given away, stolen? Woman is the capacity to arouse desire in someone. In order to achieve liberation, we need a change in the norms of sexuality, which itself necessitates the free and unimpeded taking and objectification of the body.
The personal is political, or, to know the politics of woman one must know the individual lived experiences of woman. We gather the collective reality, the throughline between women, and deconstruct it to understand the mechanisms and physics of power: how woman is object of sexuality. In a sense, woman has a [[Double Consciousness]], where she must make herself appealing to man, knowing how the world around her views her while also being an individual with her own understanding of the world.
Women have [[Exchange-Value]]s under patriarchy, they appear in the form of the [[Commodity]]. Their meaning becomes *object* enforced by the *social world*. Women's sexuality is fetishized, much like the commodity, made to appear as real and material as the object itself.
Women's bodies possess no less real desirability, or probably, desire

MacKinnon mostly describes sexuality under a discursive ([[Discourse]]) framework, in which we all come together to uphold the sexualized subject "woman" and the objective male reality. 
MacKinnon provides further analysis, this time of the state, through feminist politics and theory. The state can be understood as male [[Hegemony]], enforced and made objective by itself. It naturalizes itself by posing itself as natural, objective, and even rational. The consolidation of male power is anything but natural; it is systemic. Male power becomes power.

On *sexual harassment*, MacKinnon argued that it is not only an individual violation of one body (the body of the woman) but a violation of woman, the protected class, as a whole. As such the issue of sexual harassment resembles an issue of civil rights. It seems today we are in a MacKinnon world where this is recognized, whereas before sexual harassment was just written off.
