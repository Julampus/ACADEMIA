## Shulamith Firestone, “Love”
Women, the second sex, have been forgotten throughout history. Culture is constructed by men, and so the predominant culture is male culture. The "emotional" women are not valued in a culture that has been constructed to not value such women.

Love is a situation of total emotional vulnerability - of selfishness by which the individual wants to absorb another (re; Aristophanes; Symposium) - love, then, is an exchange of selves (for one cannot be absorbed (except through force) unless willingly)
Love is destructive in its inequality (in envy and obsession; i.e. Astora)

Seemingly, once a child is growing, at least a young boy, the degradation felt by the first rejection of love is projected to woman. For the young girl, the father - the identity giver; superego - is what is idolized, as approval is constantly vied for. The mother's love is conditional

Of bending down to the lower caste - woman - one must idolize one woman in particular; the inverse is not true for women to men, as women seek to psych men out into loving them
"Falling in Love" is just altering male view through idealization and glorification that voids women's place of inferiority, at least temporarily

Men and women seek vastly different things when they talk about love; this difference in power is political (the bodily vs. the attentive)

Love is felt, not thought

i. Men cannot love
	The man asks "how can i get her to love me without asking to be equal to me?" - impossible as love is unstable if it isn't equal
ii. Women have to cling to be able to be recognized and freed, briefly, from her precarious social situation
	A woman who does not achieve male approval in a male society is doomed
	A woman exchanges love for security
iii. This situation hasn't changed much
	Sexual emancipation, the revolution, was co-opted by men, letting women's guards down and allowing them to be taken advantage of more easily - this did not change the attention that she received towards her work
Women either pursued men and were left unfulfilled, or imitated men and lost their emotiveness and care, though remained in a world that didn't accept them
