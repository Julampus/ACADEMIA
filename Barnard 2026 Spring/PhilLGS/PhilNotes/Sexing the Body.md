Intersex bodies are understood to be variations of nature's "intention," obscuring a true, hidden sex beneath difference (hormonal, gonadal, physiological, [[chromosome]]al). It is, however, difficult to say that this is necessarily the case. Sexologists tested a number of different intersex children of varying types. 
For people born with a phallic clitoris and vaginal opening and/or functional ovaries, reproduction is prioritized over sexual functionality. The clitoris is excised or severely reduced and the baby is declared female. 
If the child is born without feminizing hormone, an androgen insensitivity, and/or has a small enough phallus, a neo-vagina is created (often involving removing the scrotum and boring a new vagina into the body, not unlike transgender vaginoplasty during SRS). In cases where children are born with both functional testes and ovaries (so-called true hermaphrodites), it is usually up to the doctor's aesthetic preferences. In fact, with clitoral length, it is not standardized whatsoever whether to reduce the clitoris or seal the vaginal opening.
