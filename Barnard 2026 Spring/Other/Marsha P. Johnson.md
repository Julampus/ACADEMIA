From Biography: *Marsha* by Tourmaline
## BIOGRAPHY
Marsha, born in a large family in Elizabeth NJ in the mid 1900's, was rarely exposed to queerness until her teenaged years. Yet her rebellion to the status-quot was expressed in other ways: embracing femininity when no one was looking (makeup, clothes, etc.), visiting NY drag balls on weekends, embodying the "dumb blonde" archetype. She had a complicated, yet supportive, relationship to her family (her mother would support her career and activism but be against her homosexuality, beating her and ripping her drag off). She could only come to understand her identity through the labels of "gay" and "transvestite."

Living in New York, Marsha initially worked at a relatively progressive cafe to earn money. It was known for being relatively progressive and was popular in the queer community at the time. This, of course, didn't earn enough money to live in Manhattan at the time. Marsha, along with her 13 year old friend Sylvia Rivera (whose father was absent and whose mother killed herself at age 3), began working on the streets. Though the red light district of NYC was initially SoHo, the neighborhood was quickly gentrified. 42nd street, Times Square (with the advent of *The New York Times* moving here), became increasingly popular as wealthy people moved to SoHo, leaving Brownstones as cheap hotels for Queens to work the streets. Frequent police raids would make this type of work significantly hard, though some could earn 1,000/hr in today's cash. 

She would later be found dead in the Hudson, a search for a killer not beginning until decades later. It was initially labeled as a suicide.
