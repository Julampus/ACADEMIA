#philosophy 

To maximize pleasure and avoid pain*
Took a [[mechanistic]], [[atom]]ic view of the world (raises the question of [[Free Will]])
Advocated for *ataraxia* - tranquility achieved through applying philosophy to life, similar to wu wei ([[Kongzi (孔子)]])

Diverged from [[Cynicism]] in the amount of asceticism and the reason

\*Epicurus wasn't posing a hedonistic concept of the world - an ethic - but rather intended to emphasize the importance and fundamentality of pleasure in all of our actions
	The most pleasurable things are not those luxuries - expensive meals, designer handbags, etc. - but rather the things that provide us with the least hardship - bread and water. We ought to live in accordance with these things 
	"Only vain and worthless things are hard to get" - though this has a beautiful message, I struggle to agree with this claim on all measures. For instance, seeking medical transition is insanely hard to get (mostly due to social-judicial factors, maybe Epicurus was speaking of material existence?) - and yet it is mostly for prudential and honorable reasons...
