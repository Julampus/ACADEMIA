#philosophy 

A mathematician and philosopher, one of the founding fathers of calculus, the other being Newton.

An Empiricist ([[Empiricism]]) though Christian, struggled to reconcile the existence with God as good and a deterministic outlook of the world through science and the mathematics. Defined the substance as a monad (something like an infinitely divisible atom) - characteristic of the infinite division that calculus predicates on.
Also established an [[Analytic Philosophy]] which argues that true propositions are always predicate-subject propositions. He takes this further, arguing that each subject as an infinite sum of predicates that individuate it from the rest (containing all past, present, and future statements), and the infinitude of which only God can comprehend. 

A polymath who struggled to reconcile theology, mathematics, philosophy, empiricsm, and idealism (you can't!)
