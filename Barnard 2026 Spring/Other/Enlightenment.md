#philosophy 

Late 17th to the 18th century
A philosophical movement birthed after the [[Renaissance]] that was defined by the upheaval of [[Empiricism]], [[Rationalism]], [[Skepticism]], Political and Social philosophy, and Atheism/Deism.

The reaction to the purely rationalist, empiricist, reductionist ([[reductionism]]), and mechanistic ([[mechanism]]) view was [[Romanticism]].

Re: [[Lecture - The Revolution to Come]] - The enlightenment acted just as much as a revolution away from the totality of religious authority inherited from the medieval and [[Renaissance]] eras in Europe as it was a revolution towards the authoritarianism of Fascism and Stalinism, both of which embraced certain parts of the enlightenment's emphasis on science, rationalism, and empiricism, while rejecting its own rejection of emotion, irrationality, absolute and concentrated power, and the impulses (e.g. race science was constructed to affirm the growing distrust and mob-rule mentality)
Yet the enlightenment remained self-critical. It is an over-simplification and, in some ways, a romanticization to say that the enlightenment, if it did exist as anything more than a social trend, was an emphasis on rationalism and rationalism alone. It was just as politically complex, transgressive, and controversial as the ideas it espoused are today.
