## Philosophy of Mind
Birthed from [[Analytic Philosophy]]
*The boundaries of philosophy and neuroscience dissolve...*
Inquiry of mind originates in the ancient tradition, with the canonical history tracing it through [[Augustine of Hippo]]'s and then [[René Descartes]]' evil deceivers (and perhaps [[Mozi]]'s butterfly). The question of whether a reductionist, physicalist, materialist description of the brain in the 20th/21st century (birthing from the questions of meaning making in the philosophy of [[Language]]) can capture the subjectivist notions of experience: colour, camouflage, echolocation, somatosensation, vision (in its many forms), audition, balance, acceleration, etc. (intersects with [[Chapter 41 - Sensory Systems]]). A shift from a behavioralist notion (prioritizing psychology/psychoanalysis) towards a neurobiological framework was the largest theoretical movement in this field in the 20th century ([[paradigm shift]])

Some argue "folk psychologies" have been developed to describe the intentional (outward facing) feelings of desire as well as the phenomenal, internal *qualia* of hunger, pain, etc. - if we come to understand the brain in physicalist notions, these ideas will cease to be just as demons ceased to be the cause of mental illness.

Thought experiments include "Mary's Room" (seeing red for the first time; do you learn something new?), what its like to be a bat/butterfly, zombiehood, etc.
