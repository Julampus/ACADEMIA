#philosophy 

As [[Jeremy Bentham]] envisioned it, Utilitarianism is the maximization of *utility* - which some measure in "[[Utils]]"; that is, the amount of pleasure and reduction of suffering. Utilitarianism is envisioned as an act-based philosophy. 
As opposed to the deontological ([[Deontology]]) basis of [[Immanuel Kant]]'s philosophy which prioritizes the intentions of the actor and the coherence to universal laws, utilitarianism envisions that it is the ends that justify the act itself.

Opponents to utilitarianism argue, then, that a pig is more moral than [[Socrates]] in the utilitarian sense, so long as the pig has enough slop to eat and mud to roll around in. This is to point out that it is not only about the "[[Good (and Ren, 仁)]]" that someone does, but the [[Virtue (Arete; De 德)]]/Character of the individual (which utilitarianism ignores)

[[John Stuart Mill]] picks up on the utilitarian tradition after Bentham
