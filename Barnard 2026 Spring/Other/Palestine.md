## The First Declaration of War
*Start of the 19th century 'till 1939*
Palestinians as a national group is a relatively recent invention, though there have been majority arab ethnic groups occupying Palestine for millennia (Muslims, Christians, and Jews, in order of prevalance). The project of Zionism was first proposed by Herzl at the end of the 19th century as a solution to the global persecution of the Jewish ethnic group - Zionism being the project to establish a nation for jews. By the start of the 20th century, Palestine was a territory of individuals who mostly identified with their individual ethnic/city/familial ties rather than any nation, and a majority of the Arab states were under the rule of the Ottoman Empire. The First World War saw the dissolution of the Ottoman Empire and the claim of various Arab lands (Syria, Iran, Iraq, Palestine, Egypt, etc.), and a power vacuum opened in Palestine after over 20 generations of Ottoman rule. Palestine fell under majority British rule. 
Britain's rule, though informed by colonization, was unique in the sense that it was imposed by the League of Nations. The economic, religious, cultural, and transnational/political interests of Britain, London, and the League caught Palestine in a triple-bind of sorts. The indigenous population barely had any say in the ruling of Britain

The Barfour Declaration in 1919 first outlined Britain's interest in Palestine as a Zionist project - without mentioning the indigenous population by name, it called for the establishment of a state in the Palestinian territory for Jewish people, implying the necessity for the transfer of Palestinian people. From its inception, it recognized the necessity for use of force in removing the Palestinian people in order to establish an ethno-state of Israel. This declaration, as well as the Mandate for Palestine that the League of Nations announced, established how Palestine would be ruled. They controlled Palestine by introducing naval blockades and preventing news from getting out until the Mandate.
The population of Jews in Palestine went from 6% to 18% before the start of the 1936-1939 revolts. jews facing persecution from countries around the world, especially from the rise of Naziism in Germany, sought refuge in Palestine thanks to the Mandate. Land and capital that they had accrued from skilled labour was pumped into Palestine as they bought up land from primarily absentee landlords. Britain also pumped capital into Palestine as investment for the production of future capital. 

Palestinian representatives were exiled to the Seychelles in the Indian ocean come 1936, when an initial strike and boycott of British goods and taxes led to an armed revolt in reaction to the Peel Commission, which sought to divey a majority of the fertile land to create a Jewish state and forcibly remove indigenous Palestinians towards Jordan. This revolt lasted until 1939. Roughly 16% of the male Palestinian population had died - Britain sent roughly 1 soldier for every 4 Palestinians and completely overwhelmed the country, ending the first war on Palestine in this century. By 1948, the call to establish Israel was solidified as the world had its sympathy for the Jews.
## The Second Declaration of War
*1948*
After the Revolt of 1936-1939, the British forces overwhelmed the Palestinian uprisings and released the White Paper of 1939 which outlined Britain's control of Palestine under the house of commons. Palestinians were given barely any representation compared to the *yeshud*, or Jewish population in Palestine which was steadily growing with the popularity of the Zionist movement. 

In the years between the revolt and the creation of the state of Israel, Palestinian voices were repeatedly suppressed. The independent Arab nations, formed after the fall of the Ottoman Empire, formed the Arab League - an allegiance which discussed international Arab monarchic affairs though was largely appealing to British interests. Britain lost a lot of appeal in the mission of Zionism as it grew more comfortable with the allyship it forged with the other nations in the Arab League, and although Mandatory Palestine was initially rejected from joining, Britain soon encouraged such an addition with the stipulation that the Palestinian representative be a mouthpiece for the interests of the British economy. 

The Arab population in Palestine struggled to keep up with the international movement of Zionism, which through institutions like the Jewish National Fund helped transform previously Arab-dominated regions of land into sites of Jewish industry. An Arab National Fund was made far too late to compete with the capital being pumped into the Zionist project. Zionism's international political appeal also gained traction, especially with the rising global superpower: the United States. The Zionist project was incredibly popular in New York, which at the time had the largest Jewish population in the world. America, with its growing industry and goals of ideological spread saw Palestine and the creation of Israel as beneficial to its overall global project. The Arab communities failed to appeal to the geopolitics that would soon take precedent with as the Second World War came to an end.

The creation of the State of Israel in 1948 prompted immediate fighting - the Zionist project necessitates the forced removal of Palestinian Arabs from their homeland. Israel comprised of roughly 56% of the land of Mandatory Palestine, while the land reserved for Arab Palestine was less than 30%. Zionist paramilitary groups expulsed vast groups of Palestinians - armed uprisings couldn't compete. (The United Nations proposed the first plan to create such a state.) Palestinians became refugees, with a majority fleeing to Jordan, the U.S., Europe, or other neighboring Arab Nations. There, they impacted economies, faced persecution, and relied on refugee relief programs (mostly funded by the UN).

The Suez War, or the Arab-Israeli war, followed. The neighboring Jordan annexed the west bank, Egypt took parts of Gaza, though the Israeli military fought them both off with allied nations. This area again became the stage for the USSR and the US to fight as proxy (think Truman Doctrine). Palestinians were caught without the ability to determine the fate of their own country, their history, their people.

The next years saw the rise of Palestinian Jihadist groups such as Hamas which sought to seek independence through armed uprising - Israel would repeatedly murder Palestinians within and outside their land.
