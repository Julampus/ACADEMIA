#philosophy 

Neoplatonism arose roughly six centuries after [[Plato]]'s death, in the 3rd century CE, thanks to the revivalist Plotinus. This resurgence and simultaneous synthesis of platonic teaching with other contemporary traditions (namely [[Skepticism]]) held great influence over the theological study of Christianity, which saw a great insurgance at the same tame, and would fuel later [[Renaissance]] and [[Enlightenment]] thought, namely [[Idealism]]. Neoplatonism even inspired religious practice of platonic ideas through ritual and worship (prompted by Proclus).

Neoplatonism rejects [[Materialism]]. In fact, it goes so far as to say that, after the One (equivalent to a first causer; the essence of unity and wholeness) created the [[Mind (Nous)]] and consciousness, the body followed (after [[Form]]) as a product of the dyads formed by mind and its self-reflection. 
It is, in fact, the disembodiment of mind that brings one closer to the One and true knowledge. 

More on the Forms, they are further characterized, whereby material reality (the Realm of Becoming), through the lens of the soul, is created and informed by the Realm of Forms
