#psychoanalysis 

The Complex is a structure originating in the [[Unconscious]] that has *innate (archetypal)* and **learned (memory/image)** components. These components make a cluster of memory that appears to have its own autonomy (often to the point of appearing as a fragment of an individuals personality; this hijacking is called ==constellation==), comprehensibility, and emotional factor in the unconscious. 

They are often split into *personal* and **cultural** complexes - complexes that vary based off of who they affect (e.g. schizophrenia vs. the role of the "older brother.") Cultural complexes notably have their origins in the cultural unconscious, a local and temporally isolated manifestation of complex and image (unlike the [[Archetype]]s of the [[Collective Unconscious]]; universally human)

These complexes cause inner collisions with the ego, phasing into the consciousness seemingly on their own accord. The complex elicits some emotional response that is tied to the stimulus that incited the complex and the memory + archetype it originates in.

For instance: a person with a victim complex may have repressed trauma (perhaps from childhood) deep within the individual's unconscious. This memory of trauma merged with the archetype of a victim and resonates with the emotions that trauma induced. Its psychic energy is likely great due to its induction at a time when the psyche was weaker and because of the length of repression. This complex, when stimulated by external/internal causes, hijacks the [[Ego]] and autonomously elicits some somatic/motive response. If the subject were told they were acting irregular, they would likely get defensive; the complex often carries with it a mechanism of self-obfuscation. It is only after symptoms of the complex die down that the patient is able to say "That wasn't me! It was if it were my inner demons controlling me!" The pre-modern understanding of these occurrences (psychosis, neurosis, dissociation) was called "possession."

Complexes are often triggered by psychic damage, most often seen in patients "masking" their true desires for moral, societal, or cultural/religious reasons

This is a key point where Freud and Jung depart from one another. While both psychologists were deeply invested in the secrets that lay within the unconscious, they differed in how they approached understanding its mechanisms of interacting with the world. Most notably, Freud argued that a complex is most often triggered due to a dissonance between the desires of society and the innate desires of an individual. In this system, the individual either bends to societal pressures and is psychologically damaged, or rejects such social guardrails and is socially shunned, also leading to damage. For Jung, it is not the disconnect between pure societal and individual desires, but rather the disconnect between two internal desires: the moral desire to conform and the prudential desire to express.
