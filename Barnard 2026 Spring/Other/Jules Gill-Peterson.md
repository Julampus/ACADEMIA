The anxiety around "men not being men" has had more cultural weight than "women not being women," in recent years at least. Sure, when the woman was damsel in need of saving, the narrative of woman-in-crisis was a lot more rhetorically poignant (we see it now with the executive ordinances made in order to "protect women" from gender ideology), but these more so occupy a space of protectionism of object rather than emphasizing the experiential and self-deterministic role of woman as subject. Men in crisis means production in crisis means capital in crisis. Cultural anxiety is birthed from male-to-whatever gender presentations, as when manhood is suddenly reconstituted, or even completely deconstructed as with trans women (man is seen as the neuter, metonymic for all humanity; re: [[Simone De Beauvoir]]), we are reminded of the frailty of other sociocultural institutions (government, law, religion, economy, etc.)
