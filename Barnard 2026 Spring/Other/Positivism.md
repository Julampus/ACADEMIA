#philosophy 

Logical Positivism - A 20th century philosophy which argues that applying scientific practice ([[Empiricism]], analytics, mathematics, and logic) is the only way to achieve meaningful knowledge, notably espoused and theorized by the Vienna Circle, a massively influential group of philosophers before the 2nd world war. More predominantly concerned with the philosophy of science and of [[Epistemology]] than Comtean positivsm
	[[David Hume]] inspired this two centuries earlier, declaring that we should cast everything that cannot be backed up with matters of fact, existence, and quantity into the fire!
	We then begin to struggle to access a great deal of knowledge, and struggle to define what things are (e.g. color) - and what of the things we are unable to verify (a. la. the verficiation principle; what is a 'law' of physics, anyways?
	Additionally, science is based in assumptions from centuries ago! We are caught in the middle of an ocean and are tasked to reconstruct our ship
	This also leaves room for fallibilism, or the phrase "we just need more data!" Existence would theoretically require infinite data to accurately reflect
	Includes [[Verificationism]], Extentionism, Scientism, Physicalism, Naturalism, Realism

Comtean Positivism - Originating in the 19th century, the goal of which was to apply science to social and cultural dynamics - notably falls through due to the arbitrary and complex nature of social practices. Grew to be a humanist-like religion that sought to copy the sacraments of the church, though was ridiculed for such an endeavor.

Legal Positivism - the view that, what makes a good law goodis not its moral basis, but rather whether it is executed as intended
