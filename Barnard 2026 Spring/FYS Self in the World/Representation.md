#linguistics 
Part of [[Semiotics]]

==It is the production of meaning through== language, both symbolically (a. la. cross for christ) and descriptively (photograph of events)
It is both a stand for (description) and a stand in place of (symbol)

Stuart Hall discusses the difference between:
The usage of signs and images to communicate meaning about identity
*Intentional* - A meaning to the author is expressed by their intention
	Our private meanings enter preestablished codes and conceptual maps in order to express and represent meaning to others
**Reflectional** - A meaning that already exists in the subject; it is merely represented and reflected (like a mirror) back onto the individual (similar to the Jungian idea that the [[Ego]]-[[Conscious]] reflects the outer world onto [[The Self]]) - also called **mimetic**
***Constructivist*** - Meaning is constructed through language (it is not fixed) - to signify meaning
	Either semiotic ([[Ferdinand de Saussure]]) or discursive ([[Michel Foucault]])

==Systems of representations== - clusters of information that all come together to construct a particular thing (universals as nominal, so particular is merely made of varying attributes of thing), the movements and techniques of such ideas make up a system
	We can only conceptualize a thing based off of its relation to other things - is it flying? is it natural? is it desired?
	Language is made up of two broader systems of representations, Hall argues:
	**1.** We have a generally shared conceptual map which makes ideas translatable, the first system of representation (catagorical) - how we index and sort concepts (how we conceptualize meaning - signified)
	**2.** The second is language, which expresses these representations via. sign
	These systems are linked via. representation (system of signifiers)
These two systems are connected via. historical/cultural codes
	*Codes* facilitate the translation between conceptual maps and linguistic symbols, allowing for shared conceptual codes to be transferred across languages
	In other words, it is a bridge between signifier and signified

Visual signs 
are *iconic* in the sense that they share some resemblance to the object it is representing
Written/spoken signs are *indexical*

Linguistic signs are generally <u>arbitrary</u> representations of ideas and concepts
Meaning appears to never be fixed, as varying languages have different symbols for things that perhaps members of other languages cannot conceptually grasp within their own language. Linguistic meaning is fluid, evolving, and culturally/historically relative
