#linguistics 

Founder of [[Semiotics]] (though the term was not used in his time) - established the [[Signifier-Signified]] distinction

Argued that the meaning of signs are arbitrarily attached to their form, and also that we construct the individuation of meaning relatively. That is, based on the differences between two concepts, two members of a linguistic system (e.g. Red vs. Green). This is what Saussure calls his "system of differences" (which tend to be, fundamentally, binary)
 All meanings are produced within history and culture, he would argue - it is a constant constitution and reconstitution of meaning that [[Judith Butler]] would iterate on to understand gender
 This constant construction of language implies that language is simultaneously interpreted (and also that interpretation is characteristically imprecise - in the lost information, new meaning is grafted onto language) - reader and writer are necessary meaning makers for [[Representation]] to take place

<u>The Structuralist View</u> - Saussure divides language into two representational systems:
	*Langue* - the linguistic structure, the rules and laws that govern language (subject - object - verb and its iterations)
	**Parole** - the actual signifiers (writing, speech, etc.) that characterize a given language
Language, Saussure hoped, could be studied scientifically if we look at the deep structure of *langue*
