## Some Could Suckle over Their Shoulder
*Male Travelers, Female Bodies, and the Gendering of Racial Ideology*

Morgon discusses the myths and sciences that have been constructed since the European discourse around black and native women, and namely their bodies, entered the cultural Zeitgeist. 
The tension between a hyper sexualization of black women (long, sagging breasts; highly fecund; polygamous; fast and painless births), and their monstrous, brutish forms as they were characterized (cannibals, sacrifices, muscles, large bodies, "barbaric" language, no families, no European customs) was necessary to the institutionalization of American Slavery.

Notably, black women were described to be seductresses, removed from the Christian God's curse unto Eve of painful childbirth, and were thus seen as a separate, convergent species (Their humanity was taken away, a necessary step toward slavery).
Their long sagging breasts, as the mythos said, reconciled their reproduction with their productive, strong, "inhuman" bodies.

American racism and slavery was founded upon the existing literary and traveler note tradition (informed by European ideology surrounding gender) that hypersexualized and, simultaneously, made black feminine bodies into monsters in order to follow an economic logic of production and reproduction. Black woman were required to become the other, relative to the white body (and even further, the female body) in order to fit into the system of capital for which their enslavement necessitated.

America, a country with patriarchal everything (land ownership, patronyms, last names, founding fathers, business owners, family heads, doctors, lawyers, laborers, experts, etc.) ONLY naturalized matriarchal system when it came to the subjugation of black women. Why? Because the entire system of capital rearranged itself to fit such a design, trampling over its own virtues, in order to maximize production and reproduction. This way, the master could rape the slave - thus self consciousness is assured through the necessitation of an other.
