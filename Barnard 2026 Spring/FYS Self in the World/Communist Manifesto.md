#economics #essay 
Written by [[Karl Marx]]

Brief Summary: This is Marx's description of what he argues is the defining struggle of human society: class struggle and who owns the means of production. He argues that bourgeois society oppresses the worker though will create its own undoing by isolating themselves and empowering the proletariat, ending in a revolution whereby private property (an unstable system) and the wage system is abolished

Since feudalism, man has been divided into oppressor and oppressed, the **bourgeoisie** and the *proletariat*
	The one who owns the means of production (land, factories, machinery, capital), and the ones who are paid a wage 

Capitalism has provoked a constant bourgeois revolution of technology and "progression"
	Feudalism => Colonialism => Industrial Revolutions => Modern times
 Bourgeois society cannot help but continuously expand into other "barbaric" (his words) lands 
 As bourgeois society continues to develop, more middle-classes fall into the proletariat (artisans, craftsmen, shopkeepers, service providers, etc.)
 This progression eventually isolates the bourgeois, arms the proletarian (we use the trains to spread the revolution), and potentially leads to the unmaking of the bourgeois class whose use is now "obsolete in modern society"
 Competition between workers is replaced with unity
 Minor unions only work to divide the worker, it needs to be one combined front
 ==Abolish private property, absorb the bourgeois class==

The worker undergoes a process of [[Alienation]] from oneself, merely existing as a right arm to the machine of industry
	[[The Self]] evaporates; one cannot express individuality in a realm of monotonous action (you are not paid to have a race, age, gender, etc.)
[[Alienation]] also exists from worker to fellow man; family is purely an economic incentive (as opposed to one of kinship as with the bourgeois)


The illusion of meritocracy shatters, as we have inherited our class since feudal times (or their historical equivalent)

The price of a commodity is equivalent to the labour-value required to create it
As work becomes more treacherous, one's wage decreases (to maximize profit for the bourgeois)

*Thesis*
1. The proletariat doesn't know they are proletariat (no *class-consciousness*)
2. Capitalism obscures this fact
3. If the proletariat learns that they are the proletariat, they will overthrow capitalism 
4. Capitalism will breed its own successor
## THOUGHT_CABINET
The revolution Marx promises in this text has not been realized. Wealth disparity is even higher, workers rights may be higher, but are outsourced to slave-like conditions in factories abroad, and workers are more alienated from each other than ever.
See: "*The Master's Tools Will Never Dismantle the Master's House*" by [[Audre Lorde]] - "the revolution will not be televised"

The bourgeois controls what people want - no one asked for TikTok, for AI: we are told what we want so that we consume. We didn't choose to get iPhones, we were born into worlds that already had them
	In this sense the old tools of production are destroyed; people cannot grow their own food, mend their own homes, craft their own goods, work their own tools and machines. We are alienated from our pasts. We must consume our current tools served to us on the bourgeoisie's silver platter. 
His thesis predicates on the fact that the bourgeoisie gets weaker and the proletariat, stronger, with technological progress. The age of computers has made this obsolete; the educated bourgeois are armed with perfect brains and precision missiles, nukes, SWAT Teams, and mainstream narratives. We are still consumers, more powerless than ever. We organize our protests on Instagram.
