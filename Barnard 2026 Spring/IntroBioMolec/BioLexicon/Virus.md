#biology 

The basis of a virus is a capsule ([[Protein (Amino Acids)]]) and genetic material ([[Nucleic Acids (Nucleotides)]] - double/single-stranded RNA/DNA). Such constructs 'hijack' the molecular machinery of host cells, resulting in the proliferation of the initial genetic material and virion.
Cases of viruses are extremely common - the common cold, influenza, and herpes viruses have infected nearly every adult human to some extent. In the case of some retroviruses like [[HIV (AIDS)]], the viral genetic material incorporates itself into the genome of the host cells through an RNA reverse transcriptase

Key characteristics of viral structure, aside from capsule and genetic material, include a helix (helical peptide structure), polyhedron, a lipid envelope, and in the case of some phages, a tail and tail fibers.

There are two key manifestations of viral cycle:
*Lytic* - In which the genetic material is implanted into the host, is transcribed by the cellular machinery native to the host cell, is replicated and assembled into new virion constructs thanks to native and newly transcribed enzymes and structural elements, before exiting the cell via. lysis. Often times this cycle involves the deconstruction/recycling of other cellular components for use in virosynthesis
**Lysogenic** - In which the genetic material is implanted into the bacteria, becomes incorporated into the genome of the host, and is replicated as the host undergoes [[Cell Division (Mitosis)]]. Once incorporated, the genetic material is referred to as a provirus/prophage
==Virulent== bacteriophages kill their host cells in order to replicate further
==Temperate== bacteriophages switch between the two cycles

In terms of [[Taxonomy]], viruses, though not alive, also follow the family, genus taxonomic heirarchy,
In terms of [[Ecology]], viruses play a role in the degradation of bacteria and organic matter in environments where such material may otherwise become inaccessible (soil, trenches) or where one single, genetically uniform species overcomes a community, thus increasing diversity.
