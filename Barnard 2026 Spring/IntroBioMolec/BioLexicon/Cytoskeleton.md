#biology 
Protein networks that facilitate cell function
	Organize structure, move vesicles, involved in division and cell attachment
	Notably dynamic
All aid in cell shape

**Microtubules**
	Hollow tube of monomers of a and b tubulin - [[Protein (Amino Acids)]] - tertiary structure...
	~25nm
	+/- ends make asymmetric structure
	Functions:
	Vesicle transport
	Form flagella and cilia in [[Eukaryotes]]
	Aid in [[Chromosome]] motion in [[Cell Division (Mitosis)]]
	Radiate from ==Centrioles== in the nucleus and get more sparse near the outer bounds of the cell, near the [[Plasma Membrane]]
	Move chromosomes
[[Motor Protein]]s interact with the microtubules
	E.g. Kinesin and Dynein - walk on the tubule - carry vesicles
	Use ATP-induced hydrolysis to provide energy for conformational change
***Intermediate Filaments***
	~8-12 nm
	Provide tensile strength for structural support, like rebar - appear as monomeric filament or coiled around one another
	Not dynamic for this reason
	Anchoring [[Organelle]]s at [[Cell Junctions]]
*Microfilaments (actin)* - See [[Muscle]]
	5-7 nm
	Anchor membranes
	Cytokinesis
	Tracks for vesicle transport
	Polymers of actin monomers
	+/- ends make asymmetric structure
	Highest density near the [[Plasma Membrane]], coming towards the [[Nucleus]]

Integrin connects microfilaments to the [[Extracellular Matrix (ECM)]] and Fibronectin
Intermediate Filaments make up anchoring junctions like the desmosome, skewering the membrane and forming [[Cell Junctions]]
