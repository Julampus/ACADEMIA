#biology 

The process by which mRNA is read and translated into a polypeptide sequence in the unit of 3 nucleotides -> 1 codon -> 1 amino acid 

An *Open Reading Frame* is the sum of all codons that will be translated into protein

Transfer RNAs are vital for translation -> [[Nucleic Acids (Nucleotides)]]
	Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase binds the amino acid to the tRNA via. the hydrolyzation of ATP

*Wobble hypothesis* - states that there should be 61tRNAs, yet most organisms only have ~45, the 3rd codon is not as important for translation in some instances (e.g. inosine on the third anticodon)

Gene expression (including [[Transcription]]) occurs in the nucleoid for [[Prokaryotes]]

Ribosomes are made in the nucleolus of eukaryotes or in the cytoplasm of prokaryotes
Can translate directly into the endoplasmic reticulum, signaled by a signal sequence. The ribosome associates with the ER and the protein is ejected upon termination

aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase attaches an AA to its corresponding tRNA
peptidyl transferase in the large subunit forms the peptide bonds

<u>Reading 5' -> 3' - Writing N to C</u> - occurs with multiple ribosomes on one mRNA - polysome (1 GTP is used for every AA)
1. Initiation
	Met-tRNA bound to GTP scans mRNA for start codon (AUG), large subunit is then recruited, GTP is hydrolyzed when tRNA is at the P site to release initiation factors and to provide energy for the next step
2. Elongation
	Recognition
		Aminoacyl-tRNA enters A site with the help of elongation factor
		Peptide bonds form (catalyzed by rRNA ribozyme)
		GTP is hydrolyzed to release elongation factor, moves tRNA to next codon site, moves other tRNA to the E site
3. Termination
	Once termination sequence is read, the ribosome is disassembled and the protein is released
