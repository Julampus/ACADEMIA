#biology 
Alpha helices are widely conserved in trans-membrane proteins - often contain amphipathic regions

"Fluid Mosaic Model" - Many proteins aren't anchored in the membrane (except for to the [[Extracellular Matrix (ECM)]] or [[Cytoskeleton]])

*Facilitated Diffusion* (Passive - High to low concentration):
	Channel Protein - allow molecules to diffuse passively down concentration gradient
	Gated Channels - can close and prevent diffusion down gradient until signal is received
		E.g. K+ Voltage Gated channel - repolarizes [[Neuron]]s after an ==action potential==
			Miraculously (ty jesus) can detect size difference between Na+ and K+, selectivity domain
		Also, Ca2+ Ligand Gated Channel
	Carrier protein - binds to one molecule at a time, conformational shift releases it on the other side of the membrane
		E.g. GLUT1 glucose transporter into the cell; GLUT4 facilitates diffusion into muscle and fat (only in some cell types - insulin regulated (see [[Diabetes]]))
**Active transport** (Against the gradient - low to high):
	Primary Active Transport: ==[[Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP)]]-facilitated==
		SERCA - Ca2+ pump, pumping Ca+2 into the [[Sarcoplasmic Reticulum]] to release Actin/Myosin [[Muscle]] movement 
		Na+/K+ pump for [[Neuron]]al electrostatic potential - 3 out 2 in, negative potential on the inside. (goes against both concentration gradients)
	Secondary Active Transport - Using energy from passive transport to facilitate active transport
		Cotransport (Symport/Antiport) proteins that take advantage of a concentration gradient to go against another gradient
			The driving ion is the ion that moves with the gradient.//
		Sodium/Hydrogen antiport pump - Pumps sodium into the cell and H+ out
		Also the glucose sodium cotransport pump
