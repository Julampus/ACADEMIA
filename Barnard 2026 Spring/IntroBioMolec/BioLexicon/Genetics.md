## Mendelian
On basic, genetic heritage through reproduction
Comparing single gene characters - Dominant and Recessive alleles

*Character* - Specific heritable attribute, e.g. Phenotype (broader)
**Trait** - Differences in the specific heritable attribute e.g. tall or short, pink or white, a relative term (more specific)

Found that genes exist in pairs of two, and that traits can be dominant or recessive

Principle of Segregation: Allele pairs segregate during gamete formation

Homozygote - true breeding individual; two like alleles (p p or Q Q)
Heterozygote - an individual with two different alleles (p Q or Q p)
Monohybrid - offspring of parents with different traits (heterozygous)
Punnett squares can be constructed to wager the probability of a certain trait expression
HomoDom x HomoRec = 3:1
Het x Het = 1:2:1

Main problem of mendelian genetics is that genotype was unable to be determined EXCEPT through crossbreeding

Dihybrid crosses can be performed, comparing two characters, resulting in a 9:3:3:1 ratio

Punnett squares made for comparing individual traits, then find the product of all of them
## Non-Mendelian
*Incomplete dominance* - where a blend of allele phenotype occurs
	E.g. a red and white flower cross leads to pink offspring - self crosses lead to parental phenotypes; a matter of which transcription factor is produced (1:2:1 F1xF1cross)
*Co***dominance** - both alleles provide distinct contributions
	Neither allele is dominant/recessive - ==scale== at which the phenotype is examined is important, identical ratio with incomplete dominance (1:2:1)
***Multiple alleles*** - >2 alleles for a given gene
	E.g. [[blood]]types (AB+/A+/B+/A-/B-/AB-/O+/O-); production of varying glycoproteins on the surface of transfused Red Blood Cells)
==Epistasis== - 1 gene is dependent on another gene 
	E.g. total melanin made and melanin deposited in hair (9:3:4)
<u>Polygenic inheritance</u> - one characteristic is controlled by >1 gene
	Impacted by lots of genes acting together (height, eye color)
Pleiotropy - One gene influences multiple traits

Genetic linkage sometimes are linked, proportional to the distance to other genes
	Recombinance/total
Recombination also changes the rate at which genes are conserved
	Rate of recombination = % of recombination versus all progeny

XY are hemizygous - Mendelian
XX are homozygous - non Mendelian
	Often leads to sex linkage (exaggerating dimorphism)

Pedigree charts can be constructed for single gene phenotypes
Test cross - cross of F1 after the P parent true-breeding generation
	Testing a homodom or hetero against the homorec
