#biology 
E.g. the [[Plasma Membrane]]

The primary unit of any membrane is the [[phospholipid]] - forming fluid bilayers and bilayer vesicles
Cholesterol ([[Lipid (Fatty Acids)]]) firms up membrane at high temperatures, low temperatures prevents membranes from freezing (acts as temperature buffer)
Proteins pierce through membranes with amphipathic structure (much like cholesterol and phospholipids)

Small, nonpolar, hydrophobic molecules can move freely (==diffusion==) across the membrane (e.g. CO2) - water can still flow through
	Hydrophilic molecules cannot pass the membrane
Facilitated transport helps polar and larger molecules move 

*Passive Transport*
	Requires no energy input - directional and specific, dependent on concentration gradient
	E.g. diffusion via. gradient and simple diffusion from Brownian motion
	Or "facilitated diffusion" - allows certain polar molecules to diffuse passively
**Active Transport**
	Requires energy, often works against concentration gradients (e.g. Na/K gates in [[Neuron]]s)

==Osmosis== - the diffusion of water through a membrane (towards higher concentration of solutes, lower water potential)

[[Membrane Protein]]s allow selective molecules to pass
