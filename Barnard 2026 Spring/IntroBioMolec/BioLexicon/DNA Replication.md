#biology 
See [[translation (RNA)]] and [[transcription (DNA)]] for how RNA is read and how DNA is replicated
Organization varies across [[Prokaryotes]] and [[Eukaryotes]]

DNA Polymerase ([[Enzyme]]) adds nucleotides to 3' end of existing nucleotide strand (so the overall direction is 5' -> 3' for writing; reading is 3' -> 5')
When you hydrolyze the phosphate group from the nucleotide, it catalyzes the reaction of binding to the polynucleotide strand

*Semiconservative replication* - occurs at multiple sites on the same strand that meet up and link the new DNA strands together, copies are high-fidelity

**Replication** bubble -> the gap that is formed between two replication forks High cyclin levels create the ORC complex, present during G1

DNA Synthesis:
	**Continuous** - Leading strand following the direction of the DNA unwinding
	*Discontinuous* - Lagging strand creates multiple segments in opposition to the DNA unwinding - creates Okazaki fragments
	Varies by either side of the replication bubble

Enzymes:
	Helicase - Unwinds DNA helix
	Single-stranded binding proteins - Stabilize single stranded DNA to prevent fork from closing
	Topoisomerase - Avoids twisting of DNA ahead of replication by making small cuts
	Primase - Sets RNA primers in the 5' -> 3' direction (with writing; random placement, not nucleotide dependent)
	DNA polymerase III - DNA-dependent DNA polymerase
	DNA polymerase I - Removes primers and fills in gaps (5' -> 3') with 3 domains:
		5'->3' Writing DNA-dependent DNA polymerase
		Reverse 3'->5' exonuclease activity
		5'->3' exonuclease activity to remove primers
	Sliding clamp - Helps the polymerase stay attached to account for conformational shifts
	DNA ligase - Connects the Okazaki fragments in the lagging ends of the strands (phosphodiester bonds)

Polymerase has proofreading activity 
DNA repair also occurs via. exonucleases after transcription
Telomere repeat buffers the end of chromosomes prevent the degradation of DNA - DNA telomerase lengthens telomeres
