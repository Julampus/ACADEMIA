## Amino Acids
20 generally identified proteins, vary by R-Group
Polymerized into peptides by peptide bond (a form of an amide bond)
	Dehydration synthesis - Carboxyl group w/OH bonds to N terminus' hydrogen
#### Molecular Structure
*Amino Group* - NH3+
**Carboxyl Group** - CO2-
==Hydrogen== - H
R Group - Varies

Backbone varies by pH
#### Four groups of Amio Acid
Divided into Nonpolar sidechains Valine, Phenylalanine, Proline, Alanine, Leucine, Isoleucine, Valine, Tryptophan, Methionine, Glycine
Uncharged polar amino acids - Serine, Tyrosine, Threonine, Glutamine, Asparagine, Cysteine
Negatively charged amino acids - Glutamic Acid, Aspartic Acid
Positively charged amino acids - Arginine, Histidine, Lysine 
#### Four structures
Primary structure - "beads on a string"
Secondary structure - **alpha** helices and *beta* sheets... "local" structural changes... hydrogen bonds between the areas of the protein backbone
	**alpha helix** - H of amine bonding with O of carbonyl group (3 AA away)
	*beta sheet* - H of amine bonding with O of carbonyl group... indeterminate length (parallel/antiparallel)
	random coils also exist...
	mostly with backbone
Tertiary structure - 3D folding across amino acids e.g. cysteine bonds
     and sometimes...
     *beta sheets* in a 3D cylindrical structure form a [beta barrel]()
     chemical interactions between sidechains
     e.g.
	     charge-charge interactions (saltbridge/ionic bond)
	     hydrogen bonds between sidechains/dipole-dipole interactions
	     disulfide bonds via/cysteine - covalent
	     hydrophobic interactions
Quaternary structure - extra molecular bonds between peptides
	e.g. Hemoglobin... 2 **a** and 2 *b* subumits

***Chaperones*** are proteins that aid in protein folding - by changing environment or putting energy into the protein (detect hydrophobic regions on the outside of proteins)
	Large multiprotein chaperonins isolate proteins in an interior chamber, allow for unfolding
	Small chaperonins also do minor adjustments
Protein folding occurs "co-translationally" or during translation
#### More
*Domain* - part of protein that is <u>necessary for function</u>... e.g. precursor domain... structural subdivisions - functional definition 
	"Necessary and Sufficient for protein function"
*Motif* - a structural feature (with consistent chemical properties) to a domain's function (e.g. methyltransferases) - structural definition
	"Necessary but not sufficient for function"
	Often [[Evolution]]arily related... structure = function
**Conformational Change** - function brings with it change...

Folding is dictated by the lowest free energy possible - most entropic => most ordered form
