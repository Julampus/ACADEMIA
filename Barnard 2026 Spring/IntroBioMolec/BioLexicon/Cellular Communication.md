How a cell sees information in the environment:
	Endocrine - the release of soluble signaling molecules (e.g. [[Hormone]]s) into the circulatory system
	Synaptic - via. neurons and electrical charge
	Paracrine - nearby cells releasing soluble molecules
	Contact dependent - (e.g. [[Lymphocyte]]s like T-cells) require contact to trigger a response across cells
	Autocrine - self-signaling

Signaling molecule = ligand (what binds to the specific receptor)

Mechanism of signal processing:
	**Reception** - receiving the ligand; it binding to the receptor
		Surface reception - for hydrophilic molecules
			E.g. [[Receptor Tyrosine Kinase (RTK)]] ~60 types in humans); regulate many processes like insulin ([[Diabetes]])
				A dimer type receptor that phosphorylates tyrosine (Y) in cytoplasmic region of the receptor; autophosphorylation causes adapter proteins
				Activates the GTP-ylation of Ras oncogene -> leads to gene expression
				[[G-Protein]] - (GTPase) is a protein that bind and hydrolyze GTP (active) to GDP (inactive)
				Not using the phosphate (using energy from hydrolyzation)
			E.g. Kinase cascade ([[Signaling Cascade]]) - kinases phosphorylate the next kinase for amplification
				Can activate up to 1000x the signal/kinase and spreading it throughout the cell
			[[G-protein-coupled Receptor (GPCR)]]; ~800 types in humans) - 7-membrane spanner protein receptor, acts on associated G-protein (heterotrimeric GTPase)
				Histamine, epinephrine, adenosine, etc. are ligands
				G-protein phosphorylates cAMP (second messenger) that triggers a cellular response (no kinase cascade; also uses Ras)
				Histamine released in endocrine mechanism which activates parietal cells ([[Chapter 47 - Digestive Systems and Animal Nutrition]]) - cAMP buildup causes kinase to allosterically regulate H+/K+-ATPase to pump H+ into the lumen
			Hydrophobic receptors e.g. Steroids pass through cell membrane, binds to transcription factor that upregulates gene expression
				E.g. aldosterone promotes the expression of Na+/K+ pumps
		Internal reception - for hydrophobic molecules
	*Transduction* - converting the signal received in reception into a language the cell can act on
		A signaling pathway in transduction is an often linear path of molecules interacting with one another
		Kinases - phosphorylate proteins
		Small molecule intermediates - small molecules are generated within the cell (cAMP)
		Ion flux - channels allow ions to move in or out of the cell
	==Response== - reading the transduced signal to lead to a specific response
		Macro-response - move, die, divide, differentiate, create product
		Micro-response - gene expression and enzymatic activity
Regulation is required for cell signaling pathways to be turned off
	Negative/positive feedb3ack loops
		E.g. phosphatases (made from kinases) reverse action of Kinases
