*Or [[Political Philosophy]] under a feminist lens*

It seems an interesting question can arise with regards to whether or not "Feminist Philosophy" is a contradiction of terms.

## From [[Amia Srinivasan]]
If philosophy is epistemelogical (descriptive), and feminism is political (prescriptive), how can there be a feminist philosophy ("you cannot get an ought from an is", as [[David Hume]] said). The general conception of philosophy, after all, is that we must approach questions impersonally. 
The argument against feminist philosophy can be refuted in two ways:
1. Feminist philosophy is not a contradiction of terms - it merely identifies its [[genealogy]] of ideas, ideas which all philosophies cannot question without fundementally restructuring the axiomatic baseline of inquiry. (I.e. it is not [[Metaphysics]] -> [[Epistemology]] -> [[Ethics]], -> [[Politics]], but the reverse)
2. Feminist philosophy is politically motivated, but so are all philosophies. In fact, feminist philosophy is more virtuous because it is honest with its ideological underpinning.
