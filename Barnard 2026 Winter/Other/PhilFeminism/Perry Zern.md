## Feminist Curiosity
In her essay by the same title (*2021*) Zern discusses feminist curiosity, describing it as the tool through which feminist inquiry is conducted. As opposed to the dominant, masculinist methods of inquiry which pose questions for the sake of efficient, extractive, oppresive, individualist desires, feminist philosophy urges the listening and overall cooperation with the self, community, and the world, not so as to fulfil personal desires, but to liberate the collective from systems of oppression. 
Zern introduces two concepts of opposing curiosities:
1. Spectacle-erasure curiosity; whereby the other's self (object of inquiry), in being raised up to the position of spectacle, becomes reduced to object, and...
2. Access-disclosure curiosity; whereby the other, because of their mere existence and access, becomes an object assumed to disclose to the subject. This disclosure isn't true personal story-telling, but rather a rehearsed history to become passable, digestible, and understandable in the eyes of the subject.
Rather than simplify, feminist curiosity aims to complexify. Rather than reduce (or spectacularize), we aim to see the world for what it is and, crucially, imagine a world beyond our own.
