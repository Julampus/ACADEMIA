## Does feminist philosophy rest on a mistake?
*2015*
Srinivasan responds in this essay to the charge that feminist philosophy have received for being "unphilosopical" by virtue that it is first informed by [[Ethics]], which then informs a subsequent gender [[Metaphysics]], as opposed to traditional "hard" philosophy which goes in the reverse order (with epistemology leaning towards the hard side). Srinivasan recognizes that it is ultimately not the fact that ethics and prescriptive reasoning (i.e. [[Pragmatism]]) is the *prima facie* product of feminist philosophy, but rather that the project is viewed as one that is ultimately deceitful - deceitful in that it disguises itself as (descriptive) philosophy while it is rather (prescriptive) politics. 
Citin [[W. V. O. Quine]]'s epistemic framework of the "web of beliefs" which, briefly, argues that all beliefs, rather than acting as isolated cells in an array, are actually a set of interconnected nodes, with proximal nodes acting as foundational, near-unshakeable beliefs by which we inform our world, and the most distal of such web-nodes functioning as those fallible beliefs, prone to being knocked out of the system. Srinivasan, in conjuction with Quine, argues that though philosophy (and by extension, philosophers) like to imagine themselves as beings of "pure reason" which act not out of pragmatic, political, or otherwise personal interests, but rather for the sake of truth *qua* truth. Srinivasan argues that feminist philosophy pushes, especially when dealing with metaphysics (a field which nowadays surrounds itself with questions of "how should we look at the would?"), honesty with regards to how we imagine the world to exist. Perhaps it is the role of the philosopher to imagine and prescribe a framework for a world that, in its curent state, cannot be described, but is in a state of eminance. As Wittgenstein argued, ~"politics breeds change, philosophy leads breeds stagnation." Yet the words in which we choose to describe the world with can lead to genuine prescriptive change; it may just be the first step
Also criticises a freqeuncy of philosophers which [[Hannah Arendt]] has described as the "[[View from Nowhere]]" - or the tendency to like to believe that we are able to conduct purely rational inquiry, unmotivated by any ideology or preference for given axioms.
