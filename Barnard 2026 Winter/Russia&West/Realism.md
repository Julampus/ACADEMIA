## Political Science
The belief that culture, identity, etc. play no role in political choices. Implies that, were any other country in the same position as country X, they would do as X does.
Realists also notably argues that the political stage is in chaos, and each state acts in their own best interests
Notably denies world government agency
States are rational actors reasonably distrustful of other states 
External factors (geography, population, etc.) matter more than cultural studies
Desire for security
All states seek survival, and are in a **state of anarchy**
War is generally avoided under realism
### Offensive Realism
Distrust the status quo! It's safer! (J. Mearsheimer)
### Defensive Realism
Maintain the status quo! It's safer!
### Neorealism
States select winners and losers, and this determines the outcomes
### Neoclassical Realism
The combination of [[Informal Patronage Politics]] and psychological factors (e.g. [[Prospect theory]],[[Groupthink]], [[Operational Codes]], etc; domestic) with realist (international) beliefs

Realist systems described in 3 different ways:
1. Unipolar - one actor clearly has the most power (a **hegenom**), all states bend to its will
2. Bipolar - two states of roughly equal power share and compete, all other states are periferal
3. Multipolar - three or more states hold some political power and agency
