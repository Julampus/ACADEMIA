## On Scientific [[Explanation]]
Scientific Explanations often follow a general structure, comprised of the:
	*Explanans* - Or the explanatory statements made to describe a phenomenon (often comprised of [[Scientific Laws]] and conditions)
	**Explanandum** - The phenomenon in question that is being described
Prompts the question, what is the relationship between the explanans and explanandum?

A scientific structure generally follows a systematic proccess that involves replicability, disprovability, [[Logic]], etc.
#### Deductive-Nomological Explanations
[[Deductive-Nomological]] statements are those that logically imply the explanandum from the explanans

A scientific explanation, according to Carl Gustav Hempel, must be:
1. Relevant - this is solved by containing a [[Deductive-Nomological]] statements or inductive probabilities
2. Testable - we must be able to empirically probe the explanandum, and potentially reproduce ([[reproducability]])
3. Conceptually Distant - it cannot simply be a tautological claim that, given a box lands in X, which is contained in Y, when we ask why it landed in X, we say because it landed in Y.
#### Probabilistic Explanations
As opposed to the (deductive) [[Deductive-Nomological]] explanations, probablistic explanations are inductive. In this sense, [[Probability]] functions in a different way as a scientific explanation, yet still has potential to provide good explanations (especially in the sphere of biology, psychology, and potentially the social sciences)
Probabilistic explanations should also be testable, reelvant, and conceptually distant
