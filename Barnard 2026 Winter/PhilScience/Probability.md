#probabilistics

Classical probability assumes that the likelihood of an occurence is simply the ratio of the favored outcome over all possible outcomes, but assumes that all outcomes are equipossible (rarely the case)

Statistical probability looks and compares empirical data in order to construct a probabilistic model, though creating only approximations

There are three broad schools of thought that are employed when discussing and thinking about the metaphysical ([[Metaphysics]]) question of "What is probability".
	This question can be aided by supposing that there is a martian who comes down to earth and asks "What is this probability thing that humans are going on about?" An answer to this question should not contain the word probability in it. Note that a description of ratios on how to find probability just kicks the can further down the road. The schools of thought are as follows:
1. Frequentism - We can say that the probability is the limit of the value of a specific outcome over all total outcomes as 'n' approaches infinity, though this explanation is unsatisfying because "you mean to tell me you can just flip a coin an infinite number of times?" Also, when we think of how the word is used, it seems deeply unsatisfying that what we are talking about is the outcome of an infinite limit of events, when we say "X *will* occur with a probability of 0.7"
2. Subjectivism - Another way of describing probability is removing it as an attribute from the event in question, and rather placing the impetus of such on the subjective observer. Under this model, we can describe probability as simply "what someone is willing to bet that some outcome will happen", though this makes the difference between a probability of 0.6, 0.8, etc. arbitrary, as there now fails to be a strong baseline for where this probability lies.
3. Primitivism - We can also say that, in the field of probabilistics, "probability", "likelihood", etc. are all *primitive* terms. That is, that we have hit rock bottom of the terms we can inquire about, and the only way to grasp the term itself is to "go native", as linguists say. This gives the martian the best solution in terms of understanding, but is also deeply unintuitive.

1 & 2 give a translation of probabilistic locutions
1 & 3 describe probability as a claim about what will happen to the object, not the subject
2 & 3 give more satisfying answers in the sense that the alien grasps on a human level what people say when they mean probability
