#philosophy #language

Or - "*Context Variability*"
The concept that words and sentences have meanings that vary depending on the context - ranges from radical (no context sensitivity) to moderate (some) to limited (only the [[Basic Set]]).

The context sensitivity of a word implies the context sensitivity of the reference itself
	e.g. love of a brother, father, son, and lover all vary by their constituents, call them 'X', of love. It is likely the case that the constituent parts, which themselves are context sensitive, lead to a context sensitive whole that is love.
This like knowledge, too, seem to be contet sensitive
#### [[Variability]]
Words that have context variability can vary in:
	Referentce - Who/what is being referred to by the sentence
	|
	v
	What is said - What is meant by the words. E.g. Jill says 'I am here.' What is said is that 'Jill is here'
	|
	v
	Truth Value - the same sentence can be true in one context and false in another
Words that vary in this way are referred to as the [[Basic Set]]
#### [[Stability]]
Words that have context stability can remain stable in:
1. The cognitive and communicative role of language
2. The ability for us to remember the important aspects of a linguistic context
3. ...
#### [[Vagueness]]
Not to be confused with variability, though all vague words are context sensitive; vagueness refers to times when the question "does it really count as 'X'" can be asked. For instance, is a 16 year old a child... ehh I mean kind of. It is on the borderline of definition. Variability then comes in when such a child is placed in different contexts.
#### [[Ambiguity]]
Ambiguity does not describe context sensitivity, but rather when it is unclear whether a word contains one meaning or another. For instance, when someone says 'The bank is tall', it is ambiguous whether or not bank refers to:
1) The financial institution, or
2) The geological structure next to moving waters
#### Graduated Adjectives
Adjectives like "fast" or "good" exist on a scale ('X' is good for a 'Y', 'X' is better than 'Z'). This varies depending on the context (the class of noun being ascribed, what it is being compared to, etc.)
#### Epistemic Modals
Variability in epistemic possibility
	'The keys might be on the table'
#### Knowledge claims
Variability in what counts as knowledge
	What does it count to know something in the loccution
	'Hannah knows the bank is open'
#### Causation
Variability in what counts as causation
	"Eating the parsnips caused Bob's stomach upset"
#### [[Counterfactual-Conditional]]
When discussing what "could have happened", context matters. The referent varies depending on how we imagine things to pan out
#### Transparent/Nontransparent
Words like the [[Basic Set]] clearly are context sensitive, and are thus called "transparent", while all other words can be described as "nontransparent". 
#### Pure/Impure
This is a catagorization of words within the basic set, though it is unclear how useful the distinction truly is. Pure words contain the three qualities listed below, while impure words lack such qualities:
1) Automaticity - the word automatically refers to a reference
2) Self-sufficiency - the word refers to a reference with no other help
3) Innevitability - the word always refers to a reference
