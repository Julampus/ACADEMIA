#politics #philosophy 

There are two broader conceptions of political philosophy dubbed by [[Raymond Geuess]] 
1) "Anodyne" conception - That there lies no distinction between the political and the theoretical, the descriptive and the prescriptive; all ethics is applied, and all politics can be theoretical.
2) The "ethics first" conception - whereby [[Ethics]] is considered to be "abstract moral theory" (descriptive/normative), and politics is simply applied ethics (prescriptive)

The lineage of Political Philosophy follows two general lines and conceptions:
1) [[Liberalism]] - That politics is an idealistic, prescriptive project discussing how society should be run. Starting with [[Thomas Hobbes]] -> [[John Locke]] -> [[Jean-Jacques Rousseau]] -> [[G.W.F. Hegel]] -> (potentially) [[Karl Marx]]
	[[John Rawls]] argues there are four tasks for political philosophy:
		Practical role - that politics should aim to unite disparaging ideas in a state and unify people
		Make citizens recognize themselves as equal beings of a nation
		Make citizens recognize that politics is a rational, cumulative, and historical project
		Aim towards a reasonably just utopia for further political development
	Additionally argued that political bodies are made of three parts:
		1. Capacity for a sense of [[Justice]]
		2. Capacity for the conception of the [[Good (and Ren, 仁)]] -> Culturally relative and based upon political socialization, thus we should make the good ideal a [[democracy]]
		3. Human are susceptible to [[Reason]]
2) [[Power]] conception - That politics is a descriptive project about the distribution of power, and the exertion of will from subject to object, state to citizen. Starting with [[Niccolo Machiavelli]] -> [[Friedrich Nietzsche]] -> (potentially) [[Karl Marx]] -> [[Max Weber]] -> [[Michel Foucault]]
