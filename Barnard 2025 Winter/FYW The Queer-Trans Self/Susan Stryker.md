## My Words to Victor Frankenstein Above the Village of Chamonix
In this essay, Stryker reflects on the oppression and dehumanization of transgender people (and more often than not, transgender women), relating the relationship between cis and trans people to that of creator and creation. It is, after all, the cissexual sexology which has dominated the western medical accounts of sex, sexuality, and psychopathology of the trans subject. As a result of being both removed outside of society while having ones identity necessarily depend on this relationship to society, the monster tethered to the hate and turmoil caused by their creator, rage is born in the trans person. Stryker advocates that we utilize this rage, sublimating our oppression through such anger into productive societal and individual change. We as the monster can provide a glimpse into the horror of truth, that Nature as cissexual society has conceived it is flimsy and breakable.

Set in 1966, made in 2005 

Home of homosexuals and transvestites
A riot, gathering at Gene Compton's Cafeteria

Harry Benjamin opened a clinic in the east coast and published [[Transsexual Phenomenon]], giving trans women, former "queens," the language to describe themselves.  

The city of San Francisco demolished most of the poorer housing projects of the city and replaced them with luxury condos, forcing more people into the tenderloin and squeezing trans women out of its cheapest rooms - they would have to live on the street or in queen-organized communal housing.

"You would drink to get through the night and save the bottle to smash over someone's head just in case someone tried to assault you"

## Dungeon Intimacies
Sadomasochism (SM) analyzed as a highly local site of *poesis* (artistic transformation)
This essay is striking in the sense that it is written not under a global, generalizable framework as most philosophy, but in a highly local setting of a San Francisco BDSM dungeon. Transness, in this sense, is not merely transsexuality (which itself is poesis), but the selective transformation of the body.
Questions arise as to whether cissexual BDSM, if it exists, has this same transformative power, or if it forever conformist and merely another tool of patriarchy.
