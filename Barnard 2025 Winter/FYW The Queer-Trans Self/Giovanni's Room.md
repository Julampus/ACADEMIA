"When I was a child, I spake as a child, I understood as a child, I thought as a child.
Now I am a man, I put away childish things"
David disdains the childish (evidenced by his frustrations with his father never treating him as though he has grown up), projecting such an idea to the feminine (due to his confusing homosexual relations with a another boy in his childhood). These frustrations can be seen in the language he uses to describe the trans people he meets, the other gay people he meets, himself, and his fiancée Hella. Baldwin distinctly draws this connection between femininity and childishness in order to both construct a vignette of the internalized phobias that a gay man would harbor, as well as to emphasize the fact that femininity has been historically infantilized and made lesser than masculinity
