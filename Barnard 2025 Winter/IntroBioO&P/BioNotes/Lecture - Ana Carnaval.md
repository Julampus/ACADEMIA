What I recall from the lecture was a discussion of [[Haplotype]] analysis to determine the [[Biodiversity]] of the Atlantic rainforest in southeastern Brasil. Her research helps contribute both to the documentation of such species (including their life and [[Evolution]]ary histories) as well as to [[Conservation Biology]], as being able to understand how an organism interacts with its environment allows one to better support such organisms in the presence of certain stressors. Dr. Carnaval identified how the upper region of the rainforest has seen a much longer, consistent level of diversity, as opposed to the southern part of the rainforest (the one that is more conserved) that experienced a sort of species bottleneck event in a relatively more recent time. She did this by creating models (computational biology) to simulate the possible evolutionary patterns that could lead to the populations we see today, and comparing these with the evolutionary historical data we know about these organisms thanks to genomics.
