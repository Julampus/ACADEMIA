### 1. Amphibian Declines
Amphibian populations face declines (for the past few decades) - very sensitive to their environments (see [[Ecology]])
E.g. the frog
	Notably affect both ecosystems (aquatic and inaquatic)
	Eats small insects (act as predator), is eaten by birds, large reptiles, humans - nutrient cyclers
	Ectotherms (cold blooded)
	Sensitive to pH, oxygen levels, temperature, and the fungal microbiome
	Heavily affected by [[Climate change]], as they cannot regulate their own temperature and moisture in their skin
	Secondary consumer
	Tend to center near the equator or coastal environments
Chytrid fungi attaches to sensitive amphibian skin, disrupts the electrolytes in the body's system and leads to cardiac arrest in the amphibians
Other threats include agriculture (human disturbances), geological events, invasive species, etc.

***Questions***
How are these stressors impacting the amphibian physiology? - see chapters 40 - 48
More susceptible to fungus, drier skin, blood pressure a lot lower, toxins from the environment will damage the organism

Ecological? - [[Ecology]]
These stressors impact the ecosystem, as frogs and other amphibians are often secondary consumers. This means that there will be a boom in primary consumer populations and likely a mass decline in primary producer populations unless some other organism fills in the niche of the given amphibian. Birds and larger reptiles will also be impacted, as they lose another source of food.
Top down and bottom up relations 

Evolutionary? - [[Evolution]]
Climate change impacts amphibian physiology by disrupting the natural inclinations that amphibians have towards certain temperate and consistent environments, selecting for amphibians that are more resistant to higher temperatures and causing mass disruptions to existing populations. [[Speciation]] will occur as more niches are filled and more populations become fragmented
### 2. Coral Bleaching
Coral - [[Animalia (Kingdom)]]
	Act as ecosystem builders
	Form [[Symbiosis]] with [[Phytoplankton]], act as food source for aquatic organisms
	Once coral get stressed, plankton are removed from coral, polyps are retracted and protection efforts are initiated - mutualism
Has been increasing since the 1980's - bleaching occurs, plankton leave, loses a majority of metabolic energy, loses the ability to deposit calcium carbonate (as this is the role of the symbiote)
Population [[Trophic cascade]] occurs, as a keystone species is removed from the environment. Impacts fish, invertebrates, and organisms on land.
Bleaching white colour occurs as primary producing [[Phytoplankton]] leave
	Can be restored, though is difficult when climate pressures actively deter this
Causes:
	Changes in temperature -> lower tides -> changing of ecosystem -> expels plankton
	Ocean acidification (carbonic acid) -> corals are unable to be affixed to calcium carbonate --> damages symbiotes
	Runoff and pollution
Impacts on
	Physiology
		The increases in temperature place stressors on the coral that likely remove most upper level organisms from the environment or place general stress on the symbiotic relationship between the organisms, leading to bleaching
		Coral is a keystone species, and its eventual bleaching from the environmental stressors leads to a trophic cascade
		Evolutionarily, most organisms that are dependent on coral for survival will be selected out and only those that are more adapted for warmer climates and harsher conditions (likely more solitary conditions) will remain
