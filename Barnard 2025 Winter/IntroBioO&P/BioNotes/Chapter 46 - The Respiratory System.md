### 46.1 - The Principles of Gas Exchange
Ventilation (that is, breathing and absorption of *O2*) occurs through a thin layer of epithelial cells, as the rate of diffusion must travel through a thin medium in order for organismal respiration to be efficient. In fact, the rate of diffusion is inversely proportional to the square of the distance that air must travel through. A larger surface area or respiratory epithelial cells are proportional to a higher rate of respiration in animals.
There, O2 diffuses through cell membranes and into mitochondria, expelling CO2
In land animals, this usually occurs in a sort of 2 way street, while with fish (who have to contest with the drawbacks of diffusion through water and lower density of air, plus a higher viscosity of respiration medium) have a one way, external respiratory system (gills). 
Amphibians and certain reptiles, as well as some more simple invertebrates, respire directly through their skin
