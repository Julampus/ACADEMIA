### 45.1 - Innate Immunity: Nonspecific Defenses
Again... nonspecific
![[Innate Immune Response.png]]

Skin acts as the first layer of defense -> Protective layer from temperature, pathogens, and to separate the vulnerable internal organs from the external ones
Mucus acts as another, internal filter that allows the expulsion of harmful pathogens
At the point of the introduction of pathogens, *macrophages* (immune cells, a type of [[Phagocyte]]), destroying the threat and releasing ==chemokines and cytokines.== (messengers of the body and promote tissue repair)
**Mast cells** recognize the damage at the site and release histamine (signals inflammation)
Histamine + Cytokines trigger blood vessel dilation, semi permeability, and facilitate transport of new monocytes and neutrophils (a type of white [[Blood]] cell). Monocytes -> Macrophages and kill more pathogens
See [[Inflammation]]
*Note phage = to eat*
For viral infection, cytokines called *interferons* signal from the infected cell and cause a paracrine response and defense system to other nearby, uninfected cells
### 45.2 - Adaptive Immunity: Specific Defenses
Aka. acquired Immunity
A certain molecule that is foreign - often a protein

***[[Lymphocyte]]***
	B-Cell
	T-Cell - helper t and cytotoxic t
	NK cells
#### a. **Antibody-mediated Immunity**
B cells differentiate into plasma cells, creating antibodies
	See [[Antibodies]]
Dendritic cell (phagocyte) cause helper T cells (lymphocyte) to recognize antigen, clone a number of t helper cells, stimulate the production of B cells with surface specificity for the antigen, and then differentiate into plasma b cells to begin producing free antibodies (or become memory cells)
Or, more accutely:
Immunological memory allows for second response to antigens (thanks to memory b cells and memory helper t cell's long life) to produce far more antigens and thus, have a more significant response to a pathogenic or antigenic threat
#### b. **Cell-mediated Immunity**
T cells kill the recognized *antigen*
Helper t cells recognize antigen from virally infected cell, stimulate production of specific cytotoxic t cells -> Kill targeted infected cell
### 45.3 - Malfunctions and Failures of the Immune System
Autoimmune reactions, though often neutral, can cause lifelong harm to individuals with severe cases:
Type 1 [[Diabetes]] is an autoimmune disease in which b cells continuously target cells in the pancreas that produce insulin, preventing its synthesis

Infected lymph nodes are often a sign of illness - *lymphadenitis*
Chronic inflammatory diseases (e.g. Rheumatoid arthritis)
	Long term tissue destruction, does not shut off properly
*Sepsis*
	When pathogens are not addressed and then spread through the [[Blood]]stream
