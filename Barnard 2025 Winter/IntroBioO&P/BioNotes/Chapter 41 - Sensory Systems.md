### 41.2 - Mechanosensation and Touch
aka. Sematosensation (pain, proprioception, pressure, temperature, touch)
notably different from - The somatic system
	Reflexes, movement, control, touch
	Receptors are all over - skin, mucus membranes, muscles, tendons, internal organs, arteries
	E.g. when its cold, the erectile tissue on the body contracts to move hairs and form an insulating layer of hair around the body
	Texture, temperature, pain -> often linked to the gustation system
Vestibular System - Balance and spatial orientation
	Where your body is in space and time (notice it when it fails; i.e. vertigo -> nausea and less balance due to a buildup in ear fluid)
	Agility movement needs to be automatic in order to escape predation
### 41.3 - Mechanotransduction and Hearing
aka. Audition; Auditory System
Components of the ear:
	Inner ear - (cochlea) filled with fluid and lined with hair cells - bend when the fluid moves
	Middle ear - eardrum + tiny bones; amplifies vibrations; mammals are the only vertebrates with *3 inner ear bones* (malleus, incus, stapes) ==maybe why music is so strong for mammals==
	Outer ear - funnels sound into the ear canal
Frequency determines pitch (organisms can't hear all frequencies; e.g. wolves have a larger range of frequencies than humans), amplitude determines volume

***Mecahnotransduction*** -> Mechanical energy to neural activity

Evolved for communication, navigation, evading predation, finding food

Echolocation -> Sending sounds into the air, receiving the sounds to determine the environment
	e.g. bats and dolphins
### 41.4 - Mechanosensation and Vision
Visual System - How light is processed by the brain; organisms see and react to different wavelengths of light
The atmosphere blocks a lot of wavelengths - what an organism can see is dependent on their environment and what is [[Evolution]]arily necessary to see

***4 Classes of Photoreceptor***
*3 cones* - RGB
**1 rod** - light -> a gray scale

Humans can see between 380-750
Vertebrates tend to have a a single lens eye

### 41.5 - The Chemical Sensors: Smell and Taste
Smell - Olfaction
	Based off of smell (often used in communication - hormonal behavior)
	Varies by organism, like all other sense factors
	Tied to our health, scent can leave based off of illness
	Odor molecules (volatile scent chemicals) in the air, get caught on nose hairs, trigger action potentials in neurons
	Navigation, danger avoidance, etc. (smelling smoke, spoiled food, foreign predators, weather and information)
Taste - Gustation
	Taste pores on your tongue detect chemicals in the food
	Avoiding toxins; gaining nutrition
	E.g. Pavlov's dog -> bitter can have different associations but can be rewired thanks to culture
	Molecules dissolve saliva
	Taste and olfaction are mostly inseparable
	Savory/Sweet = seeking nutrients
	Sour/Bitter = avoids toxins, fermented or spoiled foods
