## Lab Description
During this brief lab, we testing the five major senses and how they are able to be manipulated by the environment, the body, and by biochemistry. 
For starters, with the gustatory sensation, we tasted a lemon both before and after ingesting the chemical *miraculin* and recorded our findings (the presence of miraculin removed the sensation of sour taste). Miraculin worked likely by blocking temporarily the sour chemoreceptors in certain taste buds. We also sampled 6 different liquids, each with an isolated flavor (sour, umami, sweet, salty, bitter, water/null) and gave our theories as to the origins of the psychological and behavioral responses after consuming each one (e.g. negative reaction from bitter chemicals, as it has associations with toxins; the opposite for sweet, umami...). We also ingested gymnemic tea (containing bitter-masking *gymnemic acids*) that assumedly would make sweet things taste bitter, though systematic error caused this effect to be barely noticeable
Next, we tested the olfactory senses and how they related to taste, tasting a strawberry both with and without an open airway through our noses, noting our findings (losing a floral, perfume-like quality from the strawberry once smell is lost)
We also tested the audiovisual response to the McGurk effect and recorded our observations.
We then tested our blind spot in our eyes due to the overlapping of the optical nerve on a portion of our retinas. 
We finally tested the somatosensory phenomenon of distinguishing between two poles on the fingertips, palm, and forearm - the "two-point threshold"
