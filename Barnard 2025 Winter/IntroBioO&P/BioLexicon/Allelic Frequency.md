The percentage makeup of alleles within a population, all adding to 1

The higher the starting frequency of an allele is, the higher chance it is for it to become *fixed* (present across all organisms within the population; as opposed to *lost*)
	The probability of an allele becoming fixed is equal to its current frequency
	Less random mating reduces heterozygosity (the relative amount of heterozygotes in a given population)
An ***effective population size*** is one that supposedly has completely random mating with no preference for any trait whatsoever, nor migration, mutation, nor selection
	In other words, the ***eps*** is an idealized population that experiences evolution equivalently to a population under real evolutionary pressures. Usually this population is smaller because it experiences less evolutionary pressures (other than random genetic drift) and must compensate for its slower evolutionary rate with a smaller population size
	A size of a population that loses heterozygosity at the same rate as the real population
	Effective to actual population size ratio is 2:1
Localized mating has the same effect on lower and higher level populations
An unbalanced sex ratio lowers the effective population size
