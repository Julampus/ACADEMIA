## Organismal and Evolutionary
Asexual v. Sexual Reproduction

In sexual reproduction, can occur externally (aquatic species) vs internally (e.g. mammals)

*Testes* ([[Gland]]) -> produce testosterone ([[Hormone]]), hold sperm (gametes)
	Diet, behavior, and chemical exposure change sperm count
	Steroids mimic natural testosterone, shuts down spermatogenesis (controlled by hypothalamus and pituitary gland in the [[Brain]])
**Ovaries** ([[Gland]])
	Ovulation (movement of egg from ovaries to tubes), fertilization (sperm reaches egg in tubes), development (of the blastocyst), implantation (on the epithelial walls of the uterus)

5-8 days after fertilization, the blastocyst implants on the uterine wall

Requires both healthy male and female

***Menstrual Cycle*** 
Follicular phase -> secretes estrogen, LH, FSH
Luteal phase, later on

If fertilization occurs, hCG is secreted (what's checked in pregnancy tests)
## Cellular & Molecular
*Gametes* are sex cells, they are haploid (1n; made by 2n **germ** cells)
**Somatic** cells are diploid (2n)

Ploidy refers versions (alleles) less than copies

2n cell -> 4 1n cells
Only occurs in germ cells via. gametogenesis

==4 Stages==
	Pre-miotic interphase
		G1, S, G2 (just as with [[Cell Division (Mitosis)]])
	*Meiosis I* - first miotic division (2n)
		Prophase I- Homologous chromosomes are linked via. <u>synaptonemal complex</u> (similar to cohesins) called "pairing" - Tetrad forms, two copies of maternal and paternal [[Chromosome]]s (each with two sister chromatids) - Recombination then occurs at chiasma, crossing over thus occurs (the transfer of genes at homologous locations; diversity introduced); the breaking and remaking of bonds - ==independent assortment occurs== (maternal and paternal chromosomes randomly assorted; diversity introduced)
		Prometaphase I - Spindles are attached to chromosomes
		Metaphase I - lines the chromosomes up via. miotic spindles
		Anaphase I - splits the tetrads o create haploid cells (although there are sister chromatids, there are no copies)
		Telophase I - separation and formation of cells 
	**Interkinesis** - no new S phase
	***Meiosis II*** - separate sister chromatids (functions like mitosis with 2, 1n cells)
		Prophase II - chromatids attached at the spindle
		Metaphase II - chromosomes line up at midpoint
		Anaphase II - sister chromatids are separated
		Telophase - Separation finalizes
Random fertilization also leads to diversity

<u>Non-disjunction</u>
	Tetrads in Meiosis I (resulting in 2 n+1 and 2 n-1 cells) or Dyads in Meiosis II (resulting in one n+1 and one n-1 cells) fail to split across the kinetochore
Gametogenesis occurs in sexually differentiated gonads ([[Organ]]) - spermatogenesis leads to 4, 1n cells that differentiate into sperm - oogenesis after meiosis I leads to asymmetric cell division (one large egg and a minor polar body) - meiosis II leads to second polar body and a larger ootid -> haploid egg and polar body disintegration
	Checkpoints after Meiosis I leads to oocyte stasis for years until egg synthesis is required (see menstrual cycle above...)
