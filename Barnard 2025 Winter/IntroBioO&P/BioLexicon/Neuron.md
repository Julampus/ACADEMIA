### Structures of Nervous Systems:
*Nervous system* - summation of all of the neurons, nerves, and sensory capabilities to form responses
	Different depending on the organism (e.g. fish have increased sensory for water-based movement, birds have sight adapted for flight, etc.)
	**Nerve net** (no brain) - neurons just go around the whole body (cnidarians)
	**Nerve rings** (no brain) - a more ordered nerve net with a centralized area (e.g. echinoderms)
	Brain + nerve net = more complex function (+ spinal chord is even more complex function)
	In vertebrates, densely packed nerves called *ganglia* extend from the main nervous chord

Large diversity in neurons
	Local vs. Projection neurons
	Motor vs. Sensory neurons
Multipolar - Bi polar - Unipolar - Pseudounipolar (the amount of connections) a neuron has

### Neuron Structure
![[Neurons.png]]
The *membrane potential* of a neuron describes the difference between the outside (which in its resting state is net positive) and inside (net negative at rest), measured in millivolts) often starting at -60mV -> 20 mV -> -80mV -> -60mV equilibrium
Stimulus -> Na+ gates open -> Na+ gates deactivated, K+ gates open -> K+ gates deactivate -> Equilibrium

In a grounded state, K+ is concentrated inside the cell while Na+ is concentrated outside
	Opening K+ channels lowers Vm, while opening Na+ channels raises Vm... likely due to the size difference of the ions
	This is because 3 Na+ are ejected out of the cell for every 2 K+ that enter, 1 [[Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP)]] is used

Action potentials travel faster in the presence of *myelin* (a lipid sheath that surrounds the axon with intermittent holes called "Nodes of ranvier" in the Schwann Cell Myelin)

Electricity flows from the cell body through the axon, leaving the axon terminal and being received by another neuron's dendrites
