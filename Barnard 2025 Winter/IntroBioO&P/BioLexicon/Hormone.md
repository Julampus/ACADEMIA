#biology 

A chemical secreted by [[Gland]]s in the body as part of chemical communication in the endocrine system  ([[Chapter 42 - The Endocrine System]])

### Hormones:
Luteinizing hormone (LH) - released by the pituitary, regulates reproductive systems, prompts the testes to produce testosterone
Follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) - works in tandem with LH, also released by the pituitary - prompts spermatogenesis

Cortisol - stress hormone, steroid, from the adrenal glands
Adrenaline - stress hormone, from the adrenal glands

Prolactin - facilitates lactation (from pituitary)
ADH/Vasopressin - antidiuretic hormone, conserves water, released from pituitary (posterior)

Testosterone - androgen, sex hormone that results in secondary sex characteristic (lower voice, varied fat distribution, increased muscle definement
	Plays a key role in ovulation by preparing follicles for oocyte development and maintaining healthy function of ovaries. An engineered scientific ignorance has placed the role of such a hormone as broadly unhealthy to female subjects, though this is an oversimplified view of the relationship between the endocrine and reproductive systems. The upstream relationship of testosterone to estradiol, and from DHEA and cholesterol to testosterone, help inform a complex system of chemical action that interplays to form a balanced homeostasis prime for reproductive capabilities. Context (the androgen/estrogen dichotomy), it seems, plays a larger role in how we conceptualize biology than the actual mechanisms at play. Re: Rebecca Jordan-Young's *Testosterone*

Insulin - a dimer Tyrosine Kinase - causes the Glut4 surface receptor to intake glucose into the cell
	Glycogen synthase is activated to store glucose
	Glycolysis through upregulation of glucokinase, phosphofructokinase, and pyruvate kinase transcription
	Insulin promotes synthesis of fatty acids from acetyl-Coa
