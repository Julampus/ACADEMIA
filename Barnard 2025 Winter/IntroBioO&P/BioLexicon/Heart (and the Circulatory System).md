### Circulatory Systems
*Fish* - increasing the blood pressure of a fish circulatory system puts stress on the gills
	limited gravitational forces to overcome in water
	low oxygen demands
	a simple loop linking capillaries and arteries
	2 chamber heart
[Amphibians/Reptiles]([Biology - The Dynamic Science.pdf](file:///C:/Users/sette/Documents/ClassTexts2025/Textbooks/Biology%20-%20The%20Dynamic%20Science.pdf)) - pulmocutaneous circuit for the skin and capillaries, systemic circuit
	3 chamber heart, lowers the pressures to help fragile lungs
	Amphibians can breathe through their skin, contributing to the system
	Reptiles (minus crocodiles) - the complexity has grown, allowing for larger organisms (reptiles tend to be larger than amphibians)
	Both ectotherms, "cold" blood with varying temperature
[[Animalia (Kingdom)]] - endotherms, four chambered hearts, part of the heart pumps to the lungs while the other part pumps to the body
	Left ventricle is thicker as it has to pump to the rest of the body
	If the left ventricle doesn't work, you get *congestive heart failure* - fluid will fill in the lungs
	If the right ventricle fails, there might be an issue with the consistent beating of the cardiac cycle
	Four chambered heart with two distinct systems (pulmonary and systemic) allow for two different pressures to pump (avoiding damaging lungs at lower pressures and powering the rest of the body at higher pressures)

Blood going to the [[Lungs (and the Respiratory System)]] are part of the pulmonary/pulmocutaneous (in amphibians) circuit, while all other blood goes to the systemic circuit
### Function
Takes in deoxygenated blood from the systemic circuit through the *vena cava* the right atrium (i.e. gathering place). is transported to the right ventricle (vent, or to send away) to the lungs.
	Low pressure
The left atrium takes in oxygenated blood from the pulmonary circuit, moves it to the left ventricle to be sent to the rest of the body.
	High pressure

Heart valves usually only function in one direction
### Heartbeat
To see if the heart is healthy, you use an EKG or ***ECG*** (Electrokardiogramm/Electrocardiogramm) - can see the electro cardiac activity, if there are abnormalities, it will show on the curve of the graph (notably only shows electrical signals, not contraction)
	P wave - initial contraction of atria
	QRS complex - ventricles contract, blood ejected
	T wave - ventricles repolarize and relax
Fast heartbeat  - tachycardia
Normal heartbeat - normal sinus rhythm
Irregular heartbeat - ventricular contraction abnormality
Slow heartbeat - bradycardia

*60 - 100 bpm* is normal for a resting heart rate

Nervous systems ([[Chapter 40 - Nervous Systems]]) react to change the heartrate - there is a distinct relationship between [[Neuron]]s and the circulatory system ([[Chapter 44 - The Circulatory System]])
	E.g. when under stress (fight or flight; sympathetic system), digestion and GI track circulation dorps drastically
Vasoconstriction (tightened blood vessels to reduce flow) vs. Vasodilation (increasing diameter of the blood vessels to increase blood flow; if skeletal [[Muscle]] is used for exercise, for example)
