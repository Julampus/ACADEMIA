#philosophy 

To [[Jean-Paul Sartre]], Existentialism has three defining problems:
1. Abandonment - that we are unable to rationally prove the existence of god (or in the Nietzschean ([[Friedrich Nietzsche]]) sense, that God's prevalence in society has waned and we are to console ourselves with this apparent loss)
2. Anguish - "of Abraham" as [[Søren Kierkegaard]] put it; that our actions our not only our own, but contribute to the human project. Not only the individual, but humanity is judged by our actions.
3. Despair - that we are nothing but our actions, with no preexisting essence guides us (forget a [[Tabula Rasa]], there is no slate at all!)

Some who work within or tangentially to the existentialist tradition: [[Simone De Beauvoir]], [[Jean-Paul Sartre]], [[Friedrich Nietzsche]], [[Fyodor Dostoevsky]], [[Søren Kierkegaard]], [[Albert Camus]]...

 Humanity is not species, but rather transcends it (De Beauvoir)
