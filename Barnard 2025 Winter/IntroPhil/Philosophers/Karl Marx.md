### Wage-Labor and Capital
A laborer sells their labour power (their life, measured in time) as a commodity to the bourgeoisie. The bourgeoisie is then free to spend this commodity however they wish, often to accrue more capital.

[[Capital]] is the sum of exchange values that, under social relations (i.e. the exploitation of the worker for their labour) can be put to produce more capital, more profit.
	This exploitation is necessary for its own existence, and vice versa (the proletariat cannot survive without it)
[[Commodity]]

As capital increases, technological advancements increase. This increases competition amongst laborers (as the labor becomes more unskilled) and decreases wages (potentially past the point of subsistence). And yet, capital breeds more capital

Competition gauges price... an advancement in technology will result in an increase of capital by lowering prices... soon the competitors will catch up and the prices remain lower (yet the laborer is exploited out of more) - prices, though, keep going up

The minimum wage that a wage-giver gives, averaged, is the minimum amount required for subsistence (this maximizes profit in the bourgeois)
Capital is made by processing living labour and raw materials with accumulated labour - it necessitates the exploitation of the laborer through the system of wages, by the bourgeois buying their time and exchanging it for value (a fraction of which they return to the laborer)

Capitalism is arming the proletariat as more and more labour accumulates over the years (the crumbs of what the bourgeoisie gain) - a discussion on class consciousness follows in [[Communist Manifesto]] 
### Commodities
The commodity is the material object that satisfies a human want of some kind or another
The commodity's value is split into two parts:
	The material, use-value (utility) - this is dependent on the function of the commodity and its constitutional makeup
	The social, exchange-value - this is dependent on a web of all other commodities, all related by the equivalent amalgam of human labor
		From the social exchange of human equivalent labour, we get the price of the object
		From the utility of labour, we get the utility of the object

Fetishism - "social aspects of economic goods are inherent to them
Rather, they (e.g. exchange-value) are expressions of social relations in which goods and their underlying labour are exchanged."

	Or... the more the worker works, the more miserable the worker becomes
"necessary result of competition is the accumulation of capital in a few hands"

	In this scenario, the laborer imbues (or loses) his value to the commodity
	This is through a process of externalization, by which he appropriates the external world in order to imbue his own value into to form a labor-object

"The climax of this slavery is that
