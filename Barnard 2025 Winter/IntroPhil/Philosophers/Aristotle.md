#philosophy 
![[e5f509d88673b3bf.jpg]]
Author of:
[[Metaphysics]]

Taught by [[Plato]]
Assigned I.1–2, 980a22–983a23 from [[Aristotle.pdf]]
Established a clear division of the sciences (a part of philosophy) - physics (of the heavenly bodies), meteorology (of the sub-heavenly bodies), mathematics, metaphysics
A likely tutor to *Alexander the Great*
Born in Macedonia
Supposedly first wrote literature (likely dialogues), though this has been lost to time, only his notes on lectures and from students remain
"the Philosopher" - he was referred to in the medieval period

Very systemic, his ideas would build off each other

"All men desire to know"
	Through sense data? By sight? - yet this is limited
	Knowledge and understanding experience (as opposed to art, which only describes experience, not the "why" things are)
	Sense only tells us what is, not why it is
	Wisdom is knowing what *first* caused things (not merely what is happening, as that doesn't require much reasoning faculty as it does merely noting sense (is it though?))
	Science above the other principles, as it seeks to explain the world (assuming All men desire to know)
		Seeking absolute truths (divine knowledge) is the realm of the philosophers, the ultimate sciences

The realm of philosophy deals with God, the *first* cause of things
	Interesting that the use of "God" in caps is here (usually a Christian tradition?)

Outlined that the "good" is that of the *telos* (function/purpose) - i.e. [[Teleology]]
	For humans, their *telos* is happiness, which can be obtained from the virtues

Outlined a clear cosmology of the different spheres of bodies, inhabited by each of the planets, and then a final outer sphere in which the stars, made of ==aether== inhabit (took after [[Empedocles]]' roots)

On the [[Soul]] - believed it to be eternal and the pure form of the mind, outside the bodily

Created a taxonomy (not for organisms as Carl Linnaeus did later, genus and species, but for argument, language, and description)

Believed the best city to be a small, tight knit one, where everyone contributes to their own and their neighbor

Aristotle offered that one should pursue the [[Virtue (Arete; De 德)]]s, as Socrates did, though said that the way to go about becoming virtuous is to have the right balance between to extremes - an *Aristotelian mean* - of emotions (e.g. bashfulness vs. cowardice = courage)

Established the study of logic

## Thought Cabinet
Here is a pretty clear inspiration for the focus on the contrast between sense data and what can be known, a speciation between general sciences and Philosophy. It is also where the western philosophical canon solidifies in prioritizing wisdom over bodily experience.
