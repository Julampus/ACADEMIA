#philosophy 

A British [[Enlightenment]]-era physician and advisor during the Glorious Revolution. A political and empirical philosopher

On [[labour]] - The original acquisition of product - mixing labour into an external object
Exchanges follow, labourless
On [[Empiricism]], Locke was a hardcore defender of such belief. Locke argued that we are able to use the senses and technology in order to uncover the truth of things, yet concedes that the senses only provide a [[Sign]] of what is and not the true nature of what occurs. This marked a clear shift (alongside the ideas of [[Francis Bacon]] and [[Baruch Spinoza]]) away from the [[Plato]]nic idea that the perceptual reality is an inferior version of a realm of [[form]]s (which can only be accessed through hardcore [[Rationalism]])
Main ideas include the *State of Nature* (expounding on [[Thomas Hobbes]]' depiction of such with a little more Renaissance-esque nostalgia for the "good 'ol days"), *Consent of the People* (or of the governed), [[Liberalism]], [[Freedom]] (absolute, as it existed in the state of nature), and Natural Rights (those abilities which we had in the state of nature; those rights which we give up for the protections of our right to self-preservation).
John Locke's ideas would go on to inspire the ideologies and foundational texts of the American and French Revolutions
## The Second Treatise
==EVERY MAN HATH A RIGHT TO PUNISH THE OFFENDER, AND BE EXECUTIONER OF THE LAW OF NATURE.==

#### Ch. 2 - Of the State of Nature
Establishes *The State of Nature*, a supposed era out of time where man lived equally, lived by [[Reason]], and acted under the *Laws of Nature*
	That is until someone, the criminal, steps out of line and prioritizes himself over another (in an equal society you would prioritize your fellow man equally to your self), and so the criminal ought to be punished for being a threat to humanity
Those who have been harmed are obliged to take back on equal amount of what they suffered ("an eye for an eye") - granted it is enacted upon justly ([[Justice]])
"every man, in the state of nature, has a power to kill a murderer," as every man has been wronged in the state of nature, the state of equality 
	Has the criminal been wronged? - evidently not, as they have placed themselves in opposition to the state, to mankind, to God
"absolute monarchs are but men" - they ought to be held to the same obligations as us

Did this ever happen? - "the laws of nature, do bind men absolutely" - says an interlocutor, "Hooker," until men become part of **society**

In contrast to [[Thomas Hobbes]] - that life under the state of nature is "lawless, brutal, and short" - a war against every man
#### Ch. 4 - On Slavery
"Freedom of men under government is, to have a standing rule to live by, common to every one of that society, and made by the legislative power erected in it" - that is, what is decided upon under the consent of the people - a *social contract*
	Thus, we our following our own will, as it is the will we consent upon us
	We ought not follow the wills imposed on us, the wills we (the society) do not consent to, as that goes against the natural law
People who are unhappy with being enslaved should kill themselves to have power over themselves (holy shit.) - the man cannot, in any moment, give up the power he has over his own life (even in slavery?!)
	"I confess, we find among the Jews, as well as other nations, that men did sell themselves; but, it is plain, this was only to drudgery, not to slavery: for, it is evident, the person sold was not under an absolute, arbitrary, despotical power: for the master could not have power to kill him, at any time, whom, at a certain time, he was obliged to let go free out of his service; and the master of such a servant was so far from having an arbitrary power over his life, that he could not, at pleasure, so much as maim him, but the loss of an eye, or tooth, set him free, Exod. xxi."... yikes
#### Ch. 5 - Of Property
How can men, who inherit the world from God under common dominion, end up with different property?
The answer: labour!
A man only has ownership over himself, initially
A laborer only has ownership over his labour (the value in product, in land comes from the time of the laborer and the value from themself that they imbue into the [[Commodity]], as [[Karl Marx]] would argue)
In agreeing to gold as a standard, which has no bearing to mans survival, man has consented to the unequal sharing of wealth, property, as you are no longer working for subsistence, but for surplus
Money spawns from this initial disparity in labour

#### Ch. 7 - Of Political or Civil Society
The first society: marriage between man and woman
"the rule, should be placed somewhere; it naturally falls to the man’s share, as the abler and the stronger."
So intertwined (even moreso that male and female animals) due to their dependency for copulation (it is the man's obligation to raise the kids; they are enacted in a **social contract** with one another, of which freedoms they bestow unto the other)

#### Ch. 8 - Of the Beginning of Political Societies
Consent of the majority is how the society should be run - we must act as one body

## THOUGHT_CABINET
[[Thomas Hobbes]] argued that the state of nature is a war against every man - there is coorperation in the state of nature for Locke - e.g. marriage, friendship, equality

His ideas on property in the state of nature precede [[Karl Marx]]'s - the labour is what the laborer owns vs. the laborer imbues value unto the object

Constraints on Locke's idea of property
1. No waste
2. Can only take as much as as good for other people to take

The objects become a part of you

Draws an analogy to America (in the "old world" sense) as an allegory for (or even example of) the state of nature

Money system leads to inequality - gets around the "spoiling problem", you can take as much as you want - a technological loophole

Ownership (property) is a relationship between a self and an inanimate object
