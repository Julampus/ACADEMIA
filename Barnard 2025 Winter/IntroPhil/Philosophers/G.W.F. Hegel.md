#philosophy 
Or Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel

One of the most significant and equally esoteric philosophers since [[Immanuel Kant]]


Hegel diverges from Kantian thought insofar as, while Kant argued that there exists the noumenal world ([[Noumenon]]) that is transcendent to (lies outside of) the phenomenal world we experience (and thus reconciling empiricism with idealism), Hegel stresses that the substratum of all that exists is [[Conscious]]ness. 
Consciousness all take participate in the "World Spirit" ([[Spirit (Geist)]]), which can be conceptualized as a unified consciousness (which is all things, to Hegel) that all consciousnesses summate to. Consciousness develops, in the sense of gaining more knowledge of the good ('das Absolute'), by becoming increasingly more self-aware of its own existence (and importance/centrality as the initiation of everything). 
History, Hegel argues, takes on different appearances (explaining the title "Phenomenology of Spirit) starts with the first civilization which blindly follows absolute power. It continues into Persia and Athens, with war being waged and democracy winning out. It continues further, placing God as highest. However, consciousness soon recognizes itself as the sole creator of things. Thus, history culminates as Hegel declares that consciousness is all that there is and recognizes itself as the noumenon of all things. 
The attainment of *das Absolute*, the full self-recognition of consciousness and recognition that there is only the experiential world, is called" absolute [[idealism]]."

The process by which all things develop, Hegel argues, is by [[Dialectic]]. 

Hegel warned that the concepts of religion, false belief, etc. alienate ([[Alienation]]) the consciousness from the recognition of the self by placing another being as causer.

Hegel's ideas would become hugely influential, with social movements/political groups dividing into the *Old/Right* Hegelians (conservative) and the **Young/Left** Hegelians (E.g. Feuerbach; radicals, would go on to influence [[Karl Marx]])
