## Being Your Best Self - Authenticity, Morality, and Gender Norms
By Rowan Bell
On first pass, *authenticity* is "who we really are"

On the apparent conflict between living an *authentic* self (a supposed essential, pre-existent self; see [[Existentialism Is a Humanism]]) and the feminist theory of gender social construction and the morals of certain gender roles.

It seems, with using the narratives as trans people and gender non-conforming people as examples, that there can exist a certain **authentic** gendered experience that one can aspire to uncover, one that needs to be "found" in order to be expressed. This idea can apply to not only gender and gender expression, but all forms of expression, thought, and action. We generally expressing the authentic self to be morally (that is, ethically) and prudentially (that is, in reference to self interest) "good." However, it is often the case that certain individual expressions are anything but. Think of the numerous harmful, oppressive roles that gender play. They may be authentic while still being bad. Thus, **authenticity** is not reliant on prudence or morals, but is independent of such. Morality and prudence are often considered to be *  **authoritative normativity**; that is, that they are rules that truly guide us and remain relatively consistent, expanded across all situations. *Formal normativity* is looser, and tends to be things like laws to rules in a sports game.

Bell argues **Authenticity** to have strong normative authority on par with morals and prudence

***Social authenticity*** is what results from the context of ones life to make sense of the "self." That is, the summation of culture, society, history, etcetera. that contextualize the life of the individual. There does not exist an inner self (a [[Tabula Rasa]]), but rather the self is a constant project of becoming.
