### Introduction
Woman is in precarity, and yet the eternally feminine prevails. This is a paradox, Beauvoir claims. Femininity is both in danger and always with us - women are uteruses and yet they can stop being women at the drop of a hat.

Women are other to men, inseparably linked by biological imperative (unlike the dialectics of Jewishness and Blackness, as she said). Man and woman are linked in dialectic, man as subject (with objectivity; rationality) and woman as object (with subjectivity; irrationality
This relation is beneficial for numerous reasons; in the Hegelian sense ([[G.W.F. Hegel]]), self-consciousness is affirmed through the relationships and recognition that we offer (or do not offer) to the other. Man is master, woman is slave (Yoko Ono put it quite simply...). The social and cultural conditions have also redistributed the material conditions to benefit man. Biologically, sexual drive has been prioritized (Re: The Myth of the Vaginal Orgasm; [[Notes from the Second Year]])

Men do not consider themselves sexed, to be man is to be naturalized as human subject. To be woman is to be other in relation to man, to be lacking of man, or masculinity. Oppositional sexism depends on this distinction. Society has been historically male, history is male, and so present society is male society.

### History; Chapter 1
So it is not biology that can explain the situation, the woman condition, but rather ethnology, history, and psychology in light of [[Existentialism]]. 
What we do know is that woman was not always in subjugation (e.g. with the Amazonian women; fierce warriors who rejected motherhood.) Motherhood was extremely costly in the prehistoric period, and infanticide was common. Women would struggle to survive/recover childbirth, children take up more resources, women have less autonomy while pregnant.
But humanity does not exist as merely species, but within existential situation. Their goal is to transcend themselves, to commit to life projects, to achieve virtue and glory (are there male desires or shared human desires? Are women naturally doomed to this role? Is it that women don't like glory and virtue and all these nice things, or that they simply couldn't be cause of the curse of maternity). Either way, man were able to commit themselves, contribute their [[Spirit (Geist)]] over their own lives (production), while women were left with merely the contribution to genus and the repetition of life (reproduction). Men, it seems, make futures, while women ensure that there are futures. In this sense, women's condition is like Slave to Man's Master ([[Dialectic]])
