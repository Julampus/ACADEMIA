### "Reason" in Philosophy
On [[Reason]], we have deluded ourselves into thinking that there exists a world in which we currently perceive (an incorrect interpretation of the true world due to the error and noise from our sense data). It is ridiculous to believe that there exists multiple worlds, as Nietzsche says, echoing a Parmenidean ([[Parmenides]]) though that what is around us is simply all that there is, the "what is," while the rest that we cannot experience, the "what is not," will simply never exist. 
Nietzsche also cites [[Heraclitus]] for recognizing that everything is in motion and change (and thus, that there does not exist a static unity among things)
Places an almost holy emphasis on the senses, and flips [[Reason]] on its head, especially coming the century after the [[Enlightenment]] placed a great emphasis on such as the method to a higher, purer state nearer to the true nature of things.
This trend has been, in a way, taken for granted ever since the idea of the true [[Form]] of things, suggesting that the world we experience with our bodies (those physical, nebulous things) is simply a false projection (like a cave-wall light projection)
